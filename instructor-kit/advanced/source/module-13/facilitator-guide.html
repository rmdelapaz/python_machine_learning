The one risk of Session 13: the test set opened at home, then "improved"

Some learners will have peeked. Do not punish it; make it visible. In the warm-up status ask "has anyone already scored the test set?" and say: "Then your card says so, and the score you report is the first one you saw." The Evaluation row rewards honesty about this far more than a polished number. And in the room, nobody tunes after Milestone 6: post the rule on the board.

Warm-up

0:00

Retrieval and Status

10 min
Five questions (with answers)
  1. Why does the fraud harness validate on May, not on shuffled folds? Answer: The model scores future months and the fraud rate drifts; validating on a later month mimics that.
  2. What is the one loop the lesson does not allow? Answer: From the test set back to tuning: once you have looked at the test score, the model is finished.
  3. Name two parts of a model card that a weak card usually lacks. Answer: The baseline on the same test set (with an interval for the model), and limitations specific to the data; also often the out-of-scope uses.
  4. What does "explanations are not causes" mean for your card? Answer: Importance and SHAP describe what the model relies on; words like "causes" or "drives" need a design that supports them (none in this course).
  5. Status: your final candidate, its CV score and the baseline's, in one line. Answer: No single answer. Listen for a candidate chosen on training data, and for anyone who has already looked at the test set: they write that in their card.
How to run it

W1 to W4 on paper, four minutes; answers in two. W5 around the room: write each status on the board grid next to the Session 12 numbers; anyone without a final candidate is your first visit.

Lesson Plan

13.2

Capstone Project

130 min
Objectives (from the lesson page)
  • Frame a business request as a machine-learning task with a decision, a target, a leakage-safe split, a metric and a baseline, written down before any modeling
  • Build, compare and improve models in leakage-proof pipelines, touching the test set once
  • Analyze errors by subgroup and explain the model honestly, without claiming causes
  • Review your model against a responsible-ML checklist and document it in a model card
  • Deliver a project that reruns from a fresh start and score it yourself against a rubric
Frame · 10 min: test once, then read the errors

Say: "Today you open the test set, once. Refit the final pipeline on all the training data, score the test set, write the number down next to the baseline, and never change the model again. Then the interesting part starts: where is it wrong, and who pays for those errors?" Read the Evaluation and Error analysis rows of the rubric. Point at the checklist on the board: "Every item gets an answer specific to your problem. 'Not applicable, because...' is fine; skipping is not."

Studio II · 40 min · Milestone 6, test once, interval, errors
  • 0:00 (3 min): each learner says their final candidate aloud, then runs the test once. Write the number in the notebook and on the board grid.
  • Work (12 min): a bootstrap interval for the test metric, paired with the baseline (labs/module-13/l13_2-capstone/bootstrap_ci_starter.py shows both kinds: paired resamples on fraud, whole weeks on demand). Choose the operating point if the problem has one (the review team's 1%, top 10 for recommendations).
  • Reference (lab solution): fraud June average precision logistic regression 0.539 (95% CI 0.408 to 0.658), tuned boosting 0.556, difference +0.017 (-0.046 to +0.077): not distinguishable; top 1% holds 35 of 64 frauds (recall 0.55, 0.43 to 0.68). Demand: units saved 5.23, row interval 4.90 to 5.53, week interval 4.68 to 5.81.
  • 0:15 check-in (2 min): one word: "interval", "operating point", "stuck".
  • Work (13 min): errors by at least one subgroup (store, category, account age, history length, segment) with the baseline's errors beside them; read the twenty worst cases.
  • 0:30 check-in (3 min): each learner writes ONE finding about where the model fails. Read four aloud. Done when the test metric has an interval next to the baseline, and one written failure finding exists.
Studio II · 30 min · Milestone 7, explain and review
  • Explain (10 min): permutation importance or SHAP on the test set; two sentences that describe the MODEL ("relies on"), never causes.
  • Review (15 min): every responsible-ML checklist item answered for the problem: leakage audit, one look, baseline, subgroups, who bears the errors, sensitive attributes, humans in the loop, explanations vs causes, privacy, monitoring and fallback. If an answer changes the system (a review step, a removed feature, a threshold), make the change and note its effect.
  • Pairs (5 min): swap checklists; each challenges one answer that is too generic.
Results coaching notes by problem (no answers aloud)

A · Demand. A sound project finds: Gradient boosting on store, product, category, month, prices, promo and holiday: 2025 MAE 8.19 against 13.42 (95% CI of the units saved 4.91 to 5.53 resampling rows; a week bootstrap gives 4.68 to 5.81); ridge with one-hot 11.29; Poisson loss 8.12.
Honest negatives and traps: Temperature adds nothing measurable once the month is in: actual 8.16, last year's for that week 8.18, none 8.19. "Same week last year" (13.81) is worse than the plain per-product mean. Errors are largest for Footwear and Camp Kitchen in units; Snow in summer is near zero both ways.

B · Fraud. A sound project finds: June average precision: logistic regression 0.539, boosting with defaults 0.477, tuned boosting 0.556 (95% CI 0.423 to 0.668); the top 1% (83 held) catches 33 / 32 / 35 of 64 frauds.
Honest negatives and traps: Tuned boosting minus logistic regression: 0.017 (95% CI -0.046 to 0.077): the two cannot be told apart, and on May validation logistic regression is as good (0.539 vs 0.532). New accounts are 8.4% of legitimate June payments but 29.2% of the false alarms: a human review step and a fast release path belong in the card.

C · Reviews. A sound project finds: TF-IDF + logistic regression: test accuracy 0.877 (95% CI 0.855 to 0.901) but negative recall only 0.660; with class_weight="balanced" accuracy 0.873 and negative recall 0.873; a threshold set on CV for 90% negative recall gives 0.909 on the test set while flagging 36.8% of reviews.
Honest negatives and traps: Bigrams barely help on this vocabulary (CV 0.860 vs 0.857). Titles alone score 0.969: legitimate if titles arrive with the review, but they restate the rating, so the card must say which was used. On 3-star reviews the model flags 20.8% at 0.5 and 64.8% at the 90%-recall threshold: unvalidated, and a caveat.

D · Recs. A sound project finds: Item-kNN 0.308 and SVD with 5 factors 0.311 against popularity 0.209 (item-kNN minus popularity 0.099, 95% CI 0.082 to 0.116); validation picks k = 5 (0.285).
Honest negatives and traps: SVD minus item-kNN: 0.003 (95% CI -0.009 to 0.016): a tie, so the simpler, explainable model is a fine choice. Content-based on activity alone scores 0.176, BELOW popularity (with a popularity tie-break 0.272): an honest negative, but it is the only answer for a new product. Popularity recommends 31 distinct products, item-kNN 161.

E · Segments. A sound project finds: K-Means on the four scaled behavior columns: silhouette by k peaks at 5 (0.47; log + scaled 0.478), seed stability ARI 1.000, half-sample ARI 0.986 to 0.996; five nameable profiles whose churn runs from 0.03 to 0.32 (never trained on).
Honest negatives and traps: Gaussian-mixture BIC keeps falling past 5 (43175 at 5, 42990 at 6, 42803 at 7): BIC alone would not pick 5, which is why several signals are needed. k = 4 is unstable across seeds (ARI down to 0.512).

Studio II · 20 min · Milestone 8, the figure and the card
  • The figure (10 min): one chart that carries the argument (model against baseline by subgroup, a capacity or threshold chart, segment profiles), with a title that states the finding, saved from code.
  • The card (10 min): Recommendation and Performance, written FROM the results dictionary (labs/module-13/l13_2-capstone/model_card_starter.py shows the pattern and the number check). The rest of the card goes home.
  • Reference (model-card lab, review triage): CV picks the balanced model (negative recall 0.849 vs 0.57); test accuracy 0.873 (95% CI 0.85 to 0.895) vs 0.745, negative recall 0.873; skops needs no extra trusted types; a hand-typed 0.877 is caught.

Checks for understanding

Workbook self-check, all twelve items, as homework with the submission or as a pair activity if the showcase runs short:

Answers: Q1 A) After every choice (features, model, hyperparameters, threshold or list size) is made on the training data, once; Q2 [ 3.475 97.525]; Q3 C) The two cannot be told apart on 64 June frauds; choose on other grounds (simplicity, explainability, cost) and say so; Q4 D) The week interval: rows in the same week share the same conditions, so they are not independent; Q5 B) On 774 held-out reviews the model finds 87% of the negative reviews (the baseline finds none), at accuracy 0.873 (95% CI 0.85 to 0.895) against 0.745; Q6 D) Customers in this segment churned more often (about 32%); the segments describe behavior, not why customers leave; Q7 {'Bend': 3.0, 'Boise': 5.0}; Q8 B) Compare each group's error with its baseline and its typical volume: Snow sells about 12 units a week, Footwear about 46; Q9 A) Hit rate at 10 by customer history length, for the model and the baseline; Q10 C) Keep it out of the clustering, then check whether the segments differ by age; Q11 D) Write the card from the results (results.json and an f-string), and check every card number against it; Q12 A) The decision and the recommendation, the main number against the baseline with its interval, one specific limitation, pointing at one figure. Full set with explanations in the Answer Key.

Differentiation

Support: learners without a final candidate take their first pipeline as final today, run Milestone 6 on it, and say so in the card: a modest, honest model meets every rubric row. Stretch: the Exemplary column: an error finding turned into a fix and measured (the worked example's closed-day rule), a system change because of a checklist answer, the optional FastAPI endpoint with a match test and a drift check.

Closing

Exit ticket (3 questions, 4 minutes)
  1. Your main result in one sentence: the metric with its interval, against the baseline. Answer: for example "June average precision 0.556 (95% CI 0.42 to 0.67) against 0.008 with no model; the top 1% holds 35 of 64 frauds."
  2. One limitation specific to your data (not "more data"). Answer: for example "only 64 June frauds, so two good models cannot be ranked" or "3-star reviews are not validated".
  3. What is left, in order, before you submit? Answer: the rest of the card, then Milestone 9: save with skops, results.json and requirements.txt, Restart and Run All, check every card number against the output, self-score on the rubric.
Submission (within one week) and what goes home
  • After class (about 55 minutes required): the rest of Milestone 8 (the card's remaining sections), Milestone 9 (save, pin, Restart and Run All, check numbers, self-score). Optional: Serve it (60).
  • Hand in the project folder: capstone.ipynb (reruns from a fresh kernel), model_card.md, one to three figures, model.skops, results.json, requirements.txt, a README (what it is, how to run it, the fictional-data line, and what help was used), and the self-scored rubric. Deadline: one week from today. Rules, late options and the scoring sheet are in final-assessment.html.
  • Bring list: there is no Session 14. Do not hand in the course data/ folder: link to pinecrest-ml-data.zip, as the lesson's Sharing Your Work section says.
