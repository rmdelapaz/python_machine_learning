Capstone Project

By the end you can
  • Frame a business request as a machine-learning task with a decision, a target, a leakage-safe split, a metric and a baseline, written down before any modeling
  • Build, compare and improve models in leakage-proof pipelines, touching the test set once
  • Analyze errors by subgroup and explain the model honestly, without claiming causes
  • Review your model against a responsible-ML checklist and document it in a model card
  • Deliver a project that reruns from a fresh start and score it yourself against a rubric
Key ideas
  • The trail guide's report: the notebook is the scouting trip; the model card is the report a manager reads in ten minutes.
  • The plan comes first: decision, user, target, prediction moment, split, metric with its business meaning, baseline, and the result that would mean "do not deploy".
  • The template is the rules of the competition: its split, metric and baseline are fixed unless you write down why another fits the decision better.
  • Every feature passes the timing test: would I know this value at the moment of prediction?
  • One harness for every candidate: cross-validation on the training data only, shaped like the real use (time-ordered where the data is).
  • A first pipeline that loses to the baseline usually has a bug. Look before you tune.
  • The test set waits. It is scored once, in Session 13, after every choice is made.
Worked example: shuffled or time-ordered?

Why does the split have to match the data? The lesson's worked example forecasts daily store sales. Here is the same kind of model scored three ways.

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.ensemble import HistGradientBoostingRegressor
from sklearn.metrics import mean_absolute_error
from sklearn.model_selection import KFold, TimeSeriesSplit, cross_val_score
sales = pd.read_csv("data/daily_store_sales.csv", parse_dates=["date"]).sort_values(["date", "store"])
per_store = sales.groupby("store")["sales"]
sales["lag_1"], sales["lag_7"] = per_store.shift(1), per_store.shift(7)
sales["dow"], sales["month"] = sales["date"].dt.dayofweek, sales["date"].dt.month
sales["store_code"] = sales["store"].astype("category").cat.codes
table = sales.dropna(subset=["lag_7"]).reset_index(drop=True)
cols = ["store_code", "dow", "month", "promo", "lag_1", "lag_7"]
train, test = table[table["date"] < "2025-01-01"], table[table["date"] >= "2025-01-01"]
print(len(train), "training store-days,", len(test), "test store-days (2025)")
2896 training store-days, 1460 test store-days (2025)
model = HistGradientBoostingRegressor(random_state=42)
for name, cv in [("shuffled 5-fold", KFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=42)),
                 ("time-ordered 5-fold", TimeSeriesSplit(n_splits=5))]:
    mae = -cross_val_score(model, train[cols], train["sales"], cv=cv, scoring="neg_mean_absolute_error")
    print(f"{name:20s} CV MAE ${mae.mean():,.0f}")
model.fit(train[cols], train["sales"])
print(f"{'2025 test, once':20s}    MAE ${mean_absolute_error(test['sales'], model.predict(test[cols])):,.0f}")
shuffled 5-fold      CV MAE $551
time-ordered 5-fold  CV MAE $659
2025 test, once         MAE $600

Shuffled folds let the model train on days AFTER the days it is tested on, and neighboring days are alike, so the score looks better than the real future: $551 against $659 for time-ordered folds. The 2025 test ($600) lands between, nearer the honest harness. Choose with the harness that matches how the model will be used, and report its number as an estimate, not a promise.

The Rubric (Lesson 13.2)

Criterion1 · Beginning2 · Developing3 · Proficient4 · Exemplary
Framing"Predict X" with no decision or user.Target and metric chosen, but the decision, the costs of errors or the prediction moment are vague.Decision, user, target, prediction moment, split, metric and baseline written down before modeling, and followed.Also states in advance what result would mean "do not deploy", and translates the metric into the business's units.
Data and leakageColumns used as they come. No checks.Shapes and missing values checked, but some feature's timing is never questioned.Every feature's availability at prediction time is checked; transformations are fitted inside pipelines; the split matches the data's structure (time, groups).Also demonstrates a leak caught (score before and after) and documents data quirks such as stockout zeros or mislabeled stars.
EvaluationOne score on training data, or accuracy on rare events.Test score against a baseline, but the test set was used for choices, or the metric does not fit the decision.Model selection with cross-validation on the training data, test used once, metric fits the decision, baseline beside it.Also an interval (bootstrap) for the test metric and an operating point (threshold, top-k) chosen from costs or capacity.
ModelingOne model, default settings, no comparison.Several models tried in a loop, without a reason for the order or the final pick.A simple model first, then a stronger one; feature work and modest tuning that each earn their place in CV.Also shows which changes did not help and stops when gains are smaller than the noise.
Error analysis and explanationNone.A feature importance chart, unexplained.Errors broken down by a meaningful subgroup; worst cases read; drivers explained as properties of the model.Also turns an error finding into a fix (a feature, a rule, a caveat) and measures its effect.
Responsible MLNot considered.A generic paragraph about fairness.Every checklist item answered for this problem: who bears errors, subgroup results, sensitive attributes, privacy, human review, monitoring.Also changes the system because of a finding (a threshold, a review step, a removed feature) and says why.
CommunicationThe notebook is the report.A model card exists, but numbers lack baselines, or caveats are generic.One to two pages that lead with a recommendation, with quantified, baseline-relative evidence, specific limitations and finding-first figures.Also readable by a non-specialist, who could act on it correctly.
ReproducibilityFails or gives different numbers when rerun.Runs, but needs cells in a special order, absolute paths or unseeded randomness.Restart and Run All works from the project folder; seeds set; model saved with skops; versions pinned; card numbers match the output.Also a README, functions with clear names, and results logged (MLflow or results.json) for each experiment.

Glossary

TermMeaning
prediction momentThe time a prediction is made; only values known then may be features
harnessOne function that scores any candidate the same way on the training data
