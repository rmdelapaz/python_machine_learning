Session 12: Capstone Kickoff and Studio I

How to run the capstone kickoff: frame Lesson 13.2's brief and rubric, test every plan in a clinic, and run a timed studio for Milestones 2 to 4 (data checks and the feature-timing table, two honest baselines and an evaluation harness, a first pipeline). The capstone reference numbers and coaching notes are in final-assessment.html.

1 lesson 3 hrs session Lesson 13.2 Session 12 of 13 Studio

How to Use This Guide

  • One lesson, no new tools. Sessions 12 and 13 are studios for Lesson 13.2 (480 minutes for a solo learner). Today: the kickoff and Milestones 1 to 4 (plan, data checks and split, baselines and harness, a first pipeline). Milestone 5 (improve) goes home; Session 13 does Milestones 6 to 8 and the showcase.
  • Your answer notes are in final-assessment.html at the kit root: for each problem A to E the steps of a sound project, the reference numbers (baselines, good models, traps, honest negative results), follow-up questions and the rubric with the scoring rules. Read the notes for the problems your room chose (the Session 11 plan photos tell you the mix). Nothing from it is said aloud as an answer: the lesson has no answer key.
  • Coach by asking. Every move in the studio is a question ("which columns do you know on the day you predict?"). The template already fixes the split, the metric and the baseline: today makes sure nobody quietly breaks them.
  • The labs in labs/module-12/l13_2-capstone/ are capstone tools, not exercises: a second honest baseline per problem and an evaluation harness with a first pipeline. Each learner runs only the part for their own problem; the solutions hold reference numbers for all five.
The one risk of Session 12: an evaluation setup that is quietly wrong

The most expensive capstone mistake is made in the first hour and discovered in the last: a random split on time-ordered data, a feature known only after the prediction moment, a test item the customer already owned, a k chosen on the test set. Nothing errors, and every later number is wrong. So the room protects Milestones 2 and 3: the feature-timing table and the harness are checked by you or a partner before anyone builds a model. A learner who leaves with a correct harness and a modest first pipeline is in better shape than one with a tuned model on a broken split.

Before the Session

  • ☐ Read final-assessment.html, at least the Kickoff traps and the reference numbers for the problems your learners chose. Group tables by problem if the room allows (two to four learners per table).
  • ☐ Run python3 labs/check_labs.py module-12: 4 passed (about 15 seconds). The folder also holds capstone_start.py, the lesson's starter template as a module, which the two labs import.
  • ☐ Traffic-light cards or three colors of sticky notes per table; a visible countdown timer; the rubric (lesson page, The Rubric) on the second screen.
  • ☐ The board, before learners arrive: the five plan-clinic questions; a log grid (learner, problem, baseline, harness score, first pipeline) for the 2:40 milestone check.
  • ☐ No downloads, no servers, no internet needed. Learners need their capstone folder with data/ beside the notebook and labs/module-12/.
  • ☐ Own problems approved in writing since Session 11 (the lesson's three checks: a decision, data and labels in data/, not an existing project).

Warm-up

0:00

Retrieval from Session 11 and a Capstone Check

10 min
Five questions (with answers)
  1. Why does serve.py rebuild the request frame with the stored training dtypes? Answer: Otherwise a None in a one-row table makes an object column the imputer does not treat as missing: a silently different score (training-serving skew).
  2. Of input drift, data quality, prediction drift and performance, which needs labels? Answer: Only performance (AUC, offer precision), and the labels arrive months later.
  3. Where does the churn threshold 0.25 come from, and what does it assume? Answer: $15 / $60: an offer pays when p × 60 > 15. It assumes calibrated probabilities, which the case study confirmed with TunedThresholdClassifierCV.
  4. In the case study, when is the test set used? Answer: Once, after features, model, hyperparameters and threshold are all chosen.
  5. Your capstone in one sentence: the decision, and the baseline number you must beat. Answer: No single answer. Listen for a decision (not a topic) and a number; anyone without one gets the first visit in the plan clinic.
How to run it

W1 to W4 on paper for four minutes, answers called out in two. Then W5 around the room, ten seconds each; write problem letters on the board. Anyone with no decision or no number is your first visit in the plan clinic.

Lesson Plan

13.2

Capstone Project

160 min
Objectives (from the lesson page)
  • Frame a business request as a machine-learning task with a decision, a target, a leakage-safe split, a metric and a baseline, written down before any modeling
  • Build, compare and improve models in leakage-proof pipelines, touching the test set once
  • Analyze errors by subgroup and explain the model honestly, without claiming causes
  • Review your model against a responsible-ML checklist and document it in a model card
  • Deliver a project that reruns from a fresh start and score it yourself against a rubric
Frame · 15 min: the trail guide's report

Say: "A guide who scouts a new route hands the club a short report: where it goes, how it compares with the old route, where it is dangerous, who should not attempt it. Your notebook is the scouting trip; the model card is the report. Pinecrest's teams will read the card, not the code, and their data team will rerun the notebook."

Put the rubric on screen and read the Proficient column of each row aloud. Then three sentences from the lesson, on the board: "Do not deploy" can be a full answer. The one loop that is not allowed is from the test set back to tuning. The template is the agreed rules of the competition (split, metric, baseline): change one only with a written reason.

Today's clock, said once: plan clinic, Milestone 2 with check-ins every 15 minutes, a break with Run All, Milestone 3 with the baselines measured in class, Milestone 4, and a stand-up where everyone reads three numbers.

Plan clinic · 20 min (Milestone 1)
  • Pairs, ideally across problems (8 min): each reads the other's plan cell and asks the five questions on the board: (1) Who makes which decision with the predictions, and how often?; (2) What is the prediction moment, and which columns are known then?; (3) Which split does the template use, and does it match the data's structure (time, groups)?; (4) Which metric, in the business's units, and what does the baseline score on it?; (5) What result would make you write "do not deploy"? Every "I don't know" becomes a line in the plan.
  • Rewrite (7 min): each learner fixes their plan cell. Circulate to the learners who had no decision in the warm-up.
  • Two plans read aloud (5 min), with permission: one strong, one that improved. Name the part that made it strong (usually the prediction moment or the "do not deploy" bar).
  • Done when someone else could read the plan and predict roughly what the notebook will contain (the lesson's own done-when).
Studio I · 40 min · Milestone 2, load, check and split
  • 0:00 check-in (2 min). Cards: green (data loaded with the template, split printed), amber (loaded, checks unclear), red (not loaded). Reds first; ambers pair with a green.
  • Work (13 min). Shapes, dtypes, date ranges, missing values by group, duplicate keys, the target's distribution, odd values; one line of conclusion under each. The relevant part of data/README.md.
  • 0:15 check-in (2 min). One word: "timing", "quirks", "stuck". Then everyone starts the feature-timing table: every column, "known at prediction time? yes / no / only as a plan".
  • 0:30 check-in (3 min). Partners swap feature-timing tables for 60 seconds each: "Which yes would you challenge?" Coaching round on the challenged rows.
  • Done when each learner can say what one row is, how many rows train and test hold, and which columns they will never use.
Studio I · 35 min · Milestone 3, baselines and the harness
  • Baselines first (12 min): labs/module-12/l13_2-capstone/baselines_starter.py with PROBLEM set to their letter. One more honest baseline on the same split and metric, and the "do not deploy" sentence written into the plan.
  • Reference (from the solution): demand per product × store 12.953, same week last year 13.810 (template 13.419); fraud velocity rule average precision 0.250, 19 of 64 frauds in its top 1%; reviews keyword rule accuracy 0.788, negative recall 0.477; recs random unowned 0.053; segments five random groups -0.023.
  • Then the harness (23 min): labs/module-12/l13_2-capstone/harness_starter.py: ONE function that scores any candidate on the training data the way the split works (rolling folds for demand, May for fraud, stratified folds with negative recall for reviews, a second hidden purchase for recs, silhouette plus seed stability for segments). The baseline goes through it first.
  • Coach: "Does any row of your validation data come from AFTER its training rows?" "Does the baseline go through the same function?" Learners stuck for more than five minutes look at the solution's structure for their problem only.
Studio I · 30 min · Milestone 4, a first pipeline
  • 0:00 (2 min): "The simplest sensible model, as a Pipeline, through your harness." One-hot + ridge; scaled numbers + one-hot + logistic regression; TF-IDF + logistic regression; item-kNN; K-Means on scaled features.
  • Work (13 min), 0:15 check-in (2 min): one word: "beats", "ties", "loses". Every "loses" gets a visit: look for a bug before a better model.
  • Reference (harness solution, training data only): demand ridge 13.128 vs baseline 13.924 in rolling folds (pessimistic: unseen months, see Q12); fraud logistic regression average precision 0.539 on May vs 0.008; reviews TF-IDF + LR accuracy 0.857 with negative recall 0.577; recs item-kNN 0.286 vs popularity 0.209 on the validation item; segments k = 5 silhouette 0.470, seed ARI 1.000.
  • Last 13 minutes: start the attempts log (a small DataFrame or results.json): candidate, CV score, spread, one line of why. Milestone 5 at home fills it.
Kickoff coaching notes by problem (no answers aloud)

A · Demand. Strong early work: Plans the order decision three weeks ahead, so only planned fields are features (store, product, calendar, price and promo as planned); adds the per product × store mean as a second baseline (12.95) and a harness that respects time.
Traps to listen for: Actual avg_high_temp_f as a feature (known only after the week); a random split of weekly rows; the 14 planted stockout zeros read as zero demand; reading the 40%-off weeks (9.7 units against 20.1 at full price) as "discounts lower sales" (they are off-season clearance).
Ask: "Which columns do you know on the day the order is placed?" · "Is a zero always zero demand?" · "Inside 2024, which weeks validate which?"

B · Fraud. Strong early work: Keeps the January-to-May / June split, validates inside it on May, names the team's capacity (1% of June = 83 payments) and measures a one-line rule (velocity) as the second baseline (average precision 0.250, 19 of 64 frauds in its top 1%).
Traps to listen for: Accuracy (0.9923 for "nothing is fraud" in June); a stratified random split that mixes months; resampling before the split; ignoring the drift in the fraud rate (1.00% in January to 0.77% in June).
Ask: "How many payments can the team look at, and how many frauds does that hold today?" · "Which month validates your choices?" · "Who is kept waiting by a hold?"

C · Reviews. Strong early work: Uses the text (and says whether titles are allowed), keeps the stratified split, reports accuracy AND the recall of negative reviews from the first harness, and decides up front what the live system does with 3-star reviews.
Traps to listen for: Accuracy alone ("always positive" is 0.745 and catches no complaint); the English stop-word list, which removes "not" (accuracy drops to 0.864); fitting the vectorizer on all reviews before the split.
Ask: "Which error is the costly one here?" · "What happens to a 3-star review in production?" · "Does your vocabulary know the word 'not'?"

D · Recs. Strong early work: Keeps leave-last-out for the test, carves a SECOND hidden purchase out of the training rows for validation, masks owned products everywhere, and measures random (about 0.05) and popularity (0.209) first.
Traps to listen for: Choosing k on the test hit rate (Lesson 11.2 shows the k curve on the test set for teaching; a capstone must use the validation item); forgetting the mask (an unmasked item-kNN scores 0.113 because 69.1% of its slots are already owned); evaluating on training purchases.
Ask: "Which purchase validates your k?" · "Can your list contain something the customer owns?" · "What does a brand-new customer see?"

E · Segments. Strong early work: Chooses behavior columns only, scales (and considers logs), compares k with several signals (silhouette, inertia, BIC, stability) and plans the stability check (seeds, halves) before naming anything.
Traps to listen for: Unscaled features (silhouette 0.545, HIGHER than the scaled 0.470, because average order value dominates: a better number from a worse clustering); all 11 numeric columns (silhouette 0.189 at k = 5); age_group in the clustering; choosing k from one number.
Ask: "Which column dominates the distances, and why?" · "Do your segments survive another seed and half the data?" · "What will you do with age_group?"

Milestone check · 10 min

Stand-up in problem order. Each learner reads three numbers: the baseline, the baseline through their harness, their first pipeline through the harness. Write them in the board grid and photograph it: it is your list for the first coaching round of Session 13 (anyone without a harness, and anyone whose pipeline loses, gets an email tonight).

Checks for understanding

Workbook self-check, all twelve items (project skills), as homework or a five-minute pair activity:

Answers: Q1 A) Each morning the two-person review team decides which of the day's payments to hold; the model ranks them; Q2 B) avg_high_temp_f, the actual temperature of the forecast week; Q3 [3, 5]; Q4 C) The model will score future months, and the fraud rate drifts; validating on a later month mimics that; Q5 D) The best k on a validation purchase carved from the training rows (each customer's second-to-last); Q6 A) The scaled ones: unscaled distances are dominated by average order value, so the higher number comes from a worse clustering; Q7 2.0; Q8 C) Look for a bug (a split, a feature, a leak in reverse) before looking for a better model; Q9 D) Say what the live system does with them and measure it (how many get flagged as negative); Q10 B) Before any modeling, in the plan; Q11 A) Name who bears the errors, keep a human review with a fast release path, and report results by account age; Q12 C) Each validation block contains months the folds have never seen, so a month effect learned from one year cannot be checked forward inside that year. Full set with explanations in the Answer Key.

Differentiation

Support: learners who arrive without a plan write it in the clinic with you and narrow the scope (one metric, one baseline, one pipeline); they still meet every rubric row. Stretch: the Exemplary column early: a leak demonstrated with before-and-after scores, a second baseline that is genuinely hard to beat, a written "do not deploy" bar in the business's units.

Closing

Exit ticket (3 questions, 4 minutes)
  1. Write your harness in one sentence: which rows train, which rows validate, which metric. Answer: for example "January to April train, May validates, average precision". Check that validation never precedes its training rows on time-ordered data.
  2. Your two baselines and their numbers. Answer: the template's and the one from today's lab, on the same split and metric.
  3. Name one column you will never use, and why. Answer: for example the actual week's temperature (unknown at order time), transaction_id (a label), age_group in the segments (a sensitive attribute; check it afterwards instead).
Homework (about 2 to 3 hours)
  • Finish Milestone 4 if it is not done: the first pipeline through the harness, beating the baseline (or a bug found).
  • Milestone 5, improve (about 90 minutes): better features, then a stronger model, then modest tuning; keep a change only if it beats the previous best by more than the fold spread. Log every attempt with its CV score.
  • Do NOT score the test set. Choose a final candidate on the training data only; Session 13 opens the test set once, in the room.
  • The lesson's warm-ups: Exercise 1 (an interval for an improvement) and Exercise 2 (who gets held) are good practice for Milestone 6; the Quick Quiz (five questions).
  • Journal: one entry on the hardest decision so far (the lesson's prompt is for the end).
Bring list for Session 13
  • A laptop with the course environment and pinecrest-ml with data/ and labs/ (Session 13's files are in labs/module-13/).
  • Your capstone notebook through Milestone 5: plan, checks, harness, the attempts log, and ONE chosen final candidate, with the test set still unopened.
  • The rubric (lesson page) and the model card skeleton, open in a tab; your journal.

If You Run Long or Short

Running long
  • Shorten the frame to 10 minutes and the plan clinic to 15; keep all Milestone 2 check-ins.
  • If Milestone 3 runs over, Milestone 4 starts at home: the harness with the baseline in it is the protected outcome.
Running short
  • The twelve self-check items in pairs.
  • Pairs read each other's harness code and try to find a row that validates before it trains.
  • Start Milestone 5's first idea in the room (one feature, through the harness).
