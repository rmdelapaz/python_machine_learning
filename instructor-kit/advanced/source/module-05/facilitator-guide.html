The #1 risk of Session 5: signs and conventions

Outlier detectors return -1 for outliers, score_samples is higher for NORMAL points, and contamination changes nothing about the ranking. Every one of these produces a plausible-looking wrong number with no error. Put the convention table on the board in the 8.6 demo and leave it up through the lab; when a learner reports an AUC below 0.5, ask "which way does your score point?" before anything else. In 9.1 the equivalent trap is scale: unscaled PCA quietly becomes "the column with the biggest units".

New notebook lesson_8_6. Steps follow the lesson's sections, then its mistakes.

  1. How rare is fraud? (2m). "Under 1% of payments. Remember the number every detector must beat: doing nothing is 99.1% accurate." 
    import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
pd.set_option("display.width", 150)
tx = pd.read_csv("data/transactions.csv", parse_dates=["timestamp"])
print(tx.shape)
print(f"fraud: {tx['is_fraud'].sum()} of {len(tx):,} ({tx['is_fraud'].mean():.3%})")
    (50000, 13)
fraud: 438 of 50,000 (0.876%)
  2. Two classic rules (3m). "One column and a threshold. The z-score uses the mean and standard deviation, which the extremes drag around; the IQR fence uses quartiles." 
    amount = tx["amount"]
z = (amount - amount.mean()) / amount.std()
q1, q3 = amount.quantile([0.25, 0.75])
upper_fence = q3 + 1.5 * (q3 - q1)
rules = {"z-score above 3": z > 3, f"IQR: amount above ${upper_fence:.0f}": amount > upper_fence}
for name, flagged in rules.items():
    print(f"{name:26s} flags {flagged.sum():5d}, fraud among flagged {tx.loc[flagged, 'is_fraud'].mean():.1%}, "
          f"frauds caught {tx.loc[flagged, 'is_fraud'].sum()} of {tx['is_fraud'].sum()}")
    z-score above 3            flags   909, fraud among flagged 4.7%, frauds caught 43 of 438
IQR: amount above $482     flags  2693, fraud among flagged 4.3%, frauds caught 115 of 438
    Both raise the fraud rate among flagged payments far above 0.9%, and both miss most fraud and flag hundreds of honest tents. Rules are the baseline, and they stay in real systems because they are explainable.
  3. Isolation by hand (3m). "Pick a random cut between the min and max; keep the side with your point; repeat. A point in a crowd needs many cuts; an odd one, very few." Draw a number line and do two cuts on the board. 
    rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
amounts = np.append(rng.normal(150, 40, size=30).round(2), 900.0)

def isolation_depth(values, target, rng):
    """Random cuts needed until target is the only value left."""
    depth = 0
    while len(values) > 1:
        cut = rng.uniform(values.min(), values.max())
        values = values[values < cut] if target < cut else values[values >= cut]
        depth += 1
    return depth

for target in [np.median(amounts), 900.0]:
    depths = [isolation_depth(amounts, target, rng) for _ in range(1000)]
    print(f"amount {target:7.2f}: average cuts to isolate {np.mean(depths):.2f}")
    amount  155.11: average cuts to isolate 7.55
amount  900.00: average cuts to isolate 1.15
    That is the whole idea; a forest does it with many random trees in all features at once.
  4. Isolation Forest vs Local Outlier Factor (4m). "A tight cluster, a loose cluster, five planted oddballs. One sits just outside the tight cluster." Show the lesson's two-panel figure after the run. 
    from sklearn.datasets import make_blobs
from sklearn.ensemble import IsolationForest
from sklearn.neighbors import LocalOutlierFactor
X_toy, _ = make_blobs(n_samples=[300, 200], centers=[[0, 0], [6, 6]], cluster_std=[0.4, 1.6], random_state=42)
planted = np.array([[1.8, 1.8], [-1.6, 1.4], [10, -2], [-3, 9], [12, 12]])
X_toy = np.vstack([X_toy, planted])
y_toy = np.r_[np.zeros(500, dtype=int), np.ones(5, dtype=int)]
iso_toy = IsolationForest(n_estimators=200, random_state=42).fit(X_toy)
lof_toy = LocalOutlierFactor(n_neighbors=20).fit(X_toy)
scores = pd.DataFrame({"isolation_forest": -iso_toy.score_samples(X_toy), "lof": -lof_toy.negative_outlier_factor_})
ranks = scores.rank(ascending=False).astype(int)
print(pd.concat([pd.DataFrame(planted, columns=["x", "y"]), ranks.tail(5).reset_index(drop=True)], axis=1))
          x     y  isolation_forest  lof
0   1.8   1.8                 8    1
1  -1.6   1.4                 4    4
2  10.0  -2.0                 2    3
3  -3.0   9.0                 3    2
4  12.0  12.0                 1    5
    They disagree on (1.8, 1.8): globally it is near things (IF ranks it 8th), locally it is much sparser than its packed neighbors (LOF ranks it 1st). Draw the LOF diagram from the lesson.
  5. contamination only moves the line (3m). "Same forest, three contamination values. Predict: do the scores change?" Then write the convention table: score_samples (higher = normal), decision_function (negative = outlier), predict (-1 / 1). 
    for contamination in ["auto", 0.01, 0.05]:
    model = IsolationForest(n_estimators=200, contamination=contamination, random_state=42).fit(X_toy)
    flagged = (model.predict(X_toy) == -1).sum()
    same_scores = np.allclose(model.score_samples(X_toy), iso_toy.score_samples(X_toy))
    print(f"contamination={str(contamination):5s} offset {model.offset_:.3f}  flagged {flagged:3d}  same scores: {same_scores}")
    contamination=auto  offset -0.500  flagged 103  same scores: True
contamination=0.01  offset -0.645  flagged   6  same scores: True
contamination=0.05  offset -0.591  flagged  26  same scores: True
    Never tune contamination to make a detector better: it cannot reorder anything. Choose the threshold from the review budget.
  6. Mistake: reading predict as 0 and 1 (2m). "Code written for classifiers counts the wrong group, silently." 
    toy_pred = iso_toy.predict(X_toy)
print("labels returned:", np.unique(toy_pred))
print("wrong 'outliers found':", (toy_pred == 1).sum())
print("real outliers flagged :", (toy_pred == -1).sum())
    labels returned: [-1  1]
wrong 'outliers found': 402
real outliers flagged : 103
  7. Mistake: the sign (2m). "score_samples is a normality score. Fed into a metric that wants 'higher = more anomalous'..." 
    from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score
print("AUC of score_samples         :", round(roc_auc_score(y_toy, iso_toy.score_samples(X_toy)), 3))
print("AUC of -score_samples        :", round(roc_auc_score(y_toy, -iso_toy.score_samples(X_toy)), 3))
    AUC of score_samples         : 0.001
AUC of -score_samples        : 0.999
    An AUC far below 0.5 is almost always a flipped sign. Negate once, name it anomaly_score.
  8. Mistake: default LOF on new data (1m). "novelty=False describes the data it saw." 
    lof_toy.predict(X_toy[:3])
    AttributeError: This 'LocalOutlierFactor' has no attribute 'predict'
    Fix: LocalOutlierFactor(novelty=True) for fit-on-one-month, score-the-next.
  9. Mistake: accuracy (1m). "Last one, and it closes the loop with the first step." 
    all_normal_accuracy = 1 - tx["is_fraud"].mean()
print(f"accuracy of flagging nothing at all: {all_normal_accuracy:.4f}")
    accuracy of flagging nothing at all: 0.9912
    Judge with ROC AUC, average precision and catches within the budget, next to a rule baseline. Draw the review loop from the lesson: reviewed cases become labels.
Three mistakes to expect, and how to coach each
  • A flipped sign. Symptom: an AUC of 0.153 in TODO 2 of the lab, or a queue that catches nothing. Coach: "Which way does score_samples point?"
  • Counting predict == 1 as outliers. Symptom: "the detector flags 98% of payments". Coach: "What does an inlier get?"
  • Tuning contamination to catch more. Symptom: a learner raising contamination and expecting a better AUC. Coach: "Did the scores change?" Then: "How many can the team review?"

Mental model to say

"Hold a hiking boot under a flashlight. From the side its shadow is clearly a boot; from straight above the toe it is a blob. Both are flat pictures of the same object, but one keeps far more of its shape. PCA chooses the angle for you: the one where the shadow is most spread out, because spread is where the differences between rows live."

Three facts on the board: unsupervised (it never sees churn), each component is a mix of all columns (not a selection), linear (straight directions; curves are Lesson 9.3).

  1. The direction of most spread (3m). "Two columns that move together. PCA turns the cloud until its shadow is widest." Show the arrow figure. 
    import numpy as np

  8. PCA before a model: test it (4m). "Scaler, PCA and logistic regression in one pipeline, so each fold fits its own. Compare with no PCA." 
    from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline

  9. Running PCA backward (3m). "inverse_transform rebuilds rows from the kept components; the gap is what they could not express." 
    for k in [2, 5, 7, 9, 10]:
    model_k = PCA(n_components=k).fit(X_train_s)
    rebuilt = model_k.inverse_transform(model_k.transform(X_test_s))
    rmse = np.sqrt(((X_test_s.to_numpy() - rebuilt) ** 2).mean())
    print(f"{k:2d} components   reconstruction RMSE {rmse:.3f}")

Three mistakes to expect, and how to coach each
  • Forgetting to scale. Symptom: one column with weight near 1.0 in PC1 (account age in the lab). Coach: "What units is that column in?"
  • Reading signs. Symptom: "PC1 is negative for loyal customers, so loyalty is bad". Coach: multiply the arrow by -1; does anything change?
  • Most variance = most useful. Symptom: expecting PC1 to separate fraud in the lab. Coach: "Which component does?" (PC2.)

Checks for understanding

Ask out loud:

  • What does explained_variance_ratio_ tell you? Answer: each component's share of the total variance.
  • When should you NOT standardize before PCA? Answer: when all columns share one meaningful unit (monthly sales, pixels).
  • Why put PCA inside the pipeline for cross-validation? Answer: each fold fits its own scaler and PCA: no leakage.
  • What is reconstruction error? Answer: the gap between a row and its rebuild from the kept components: what they cannot express.

Exit ticket (3 questions, no notes, 4 minutes)

Answers on sticky notes. Question 3 tells you how much of the Session 6 warm-up goes to scaling and to supervised vs unsupervised projections.

  1. An Isolation Forest's predict returns -1 and 1, and score_samples gives an AUC of 0.2. Write the two lines you would use instead to build a review queue of 165. Answer: anomaly_score = -model.score_samples(X) and queue = np.argsort(-anomaly_score)[:165] (then count frauds in it).
