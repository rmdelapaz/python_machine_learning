Transfer Learning

A satellite classifier from 2,000 labels and a few minutes of CPU.

10.5 · Mental model

The experienced photo editor

  • A newcomer needs thousands of photos to learn what an edge or a texture is
  • An experienced editor already sees them; a few examples per land type are enough
  • Backbone: the pretrained convolutions, from 1.2 million ImageNet photos
  • Head: the last layer; throw it away and attach your own
WHY You teach only the last step: how its vocabulary maps onto your labels.
10.5 · Four strategies

How much of the backbone may change?

  • Feature extraction: frozen backbone, train only the new head (little data; fast)
  • Fine-tuning: head plus the last block or two, small learning rate
  • Full fine-tuning: everything from pretrained weights (lots of data, a GPU)
  • From scratch: huge datasets, or inputs nothing like photos
WHY Today: the first two on a CPU, honestly compared with the fourth.

10.5 · Code

Freeze first, then the new head

ft_model = models.resnet18(weights=weights)
for p in ft_model.parameters():
    p.requires_grad = False                       # freeze first
ft_model.fc = nn.Linear(512, 10)                  # then the new head
print(f"head only    : {count_trainable(ft_model):,}")
for p in ft_model.layer4.parameters():
    p.requires_grad = True
print(f"head + layer4: {count_trainable(ft_model):,}")
head only    : 5,130
head + layer4: 8,398,858
ORDER New layers are trainable by default. Freeze after attaching, and nothing learns.

10.5 · Common mistakes

Three of five fail quietly

  • pretrained=True: deprecated, use weights=
  • A grayscale image: 3 channels expected (repeat the channel)
  • Freezing the new head too: element 0 of tensors does not require grad
  • No ImageNet normalization: 0.840 instead of 0.894, silently
  • Features in training mode: no error, wrong features

11.1

Text Classification

Teach a model to read 4,000 reviews.

11.1 · Mental model

The clerk with the tally sheet

  • A sheet listing every word customers have ever used
  • One tick per word per review: no grammar, no order
  • That is a bag of words: a fixed-length row of numbers
  • It fails on phrases: "not bad" and "bad" leave almost the same ticks
WHY Text → numbers is the whole craft. The rest is the scikit-learn you know.

11.1 · Common mistakes

A vectorizer is a fitted transformer

  • A DataFrame: inconsistent numbers of samples: [1, 3095]
  • A new vectorizer on the test text: X has 331 features
  • Missing text: np.nan is an invalid document
  • Vectorizing before the split: no error, a leak
  • A stop-word list unread: no error, a worse model
