8.6

Anomaly Detection

Know normal, flag the rest.

8.6 · Mental model

The night guard

  • Knows what normal sounds like after a few weeks
  • Anything that does not fit gets a closer look
  • Most closer looks are nothing: the point is to look in the right places
  • A model learns normal and gives every case an anomaly score
WHY You decide how many of the highest scores you can afford to check.
8.6 · Three setups

Labels or not?

  • Supervised: many labeled frauds (Lesson 2.5)
  • Unsupervised: no labels; training data may contain anomalies
  • Novelty: trained only on clean normal data
  • Point, contextual and collective anomalies: features turn the last two into the first

8.6 · Conventions

Learn these once

  • score_samples: higher = MORE normal; negate it
  • decision_function: negative = outlier
  • predict: -1 outlier, 1 inlier (never 0)
  • LOF: novelty=True to score new data
WATCH An AUC far below 0.5 is almost always a flipped sign.

8.6 · Judging a detector

Accuracy is useless here

  • Catches in the review budget: what the manager cares about
  • Time split; a rule baseline; who gets the false alarms?

9.1

Principal Component Analysis

The angle with the widest shadow.

9.1 · Mental model

The flashlight and the shadow

  • A boot's side shadow keeps its shape; the toe-on shadow is a blob
  • PCA picks the angle with the most spread: PC1
  • PC2: the widest direction left, at a right angle
  • Unsupervised · a mix of all columns · linear
9.1 · Code

Two columns, two directions

9.1 · Common mistakes

Quiet failures

  • More components than columns: ValueError
  • Gaps: Input X contains NaN
  • Forgetting to scale: AUC 0.759 instead of 0.786
  • Fitting PCA on all rows before the split: put it in the pipeline
