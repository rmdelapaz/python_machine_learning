1.2 · Code

One split is one sample: 30 seeds

def split_and_score(seed, test_size=0.25):
    X_tr, X_te, y_tr, y_te = train_test_split(X, y, test_size=test_size,
                                              stratify=y, random_state=seed)
    scaler = StandardScaler().fit(X_tr)
    model = LogisticRegression().fit(scaler.transform(X_tr), y_tr)
    return model.score(scaler.transform(X_te), y_te)

scores = pd.Series([split_and_score(seed) for seed in range(30)])
print(scores.describe()[["mean", "std", "min", "max"]].round(3).to_dict())
{'mean': 0.851, 'std': 0.007, 'min': 0.836, 'max': 0.866}
WHY Same model, same data: 0.836 to 0.866. The lead over the baseline is only about 0.016.

test_rates = {"random": [], "stratified": []}
for seed in range(30):

    test_rates["random"].append(y_te.mean())
    *_, y_te = train_test_split(X, y, test_size=0.25, stratify=y, random_state=seed)

     random  stratified

WHY Baseline accuracy = 1 - churn rate. Stratify on the target (stratify=y), never on a feature.

1.2 · Leakage

Four ways information leaks

  • Target leakage: recorded after the outcome (cancellation_reason)
  • Contamination: a scaler, imputer or selector fitted before the split
  • Time leakage: the future helps (a random split, a centered average)
  • Group leakage: the same product or customer on both sides
WHY Ask of every column: known at prediction time? Same for future rows?

1.2 · Common mistakes

Two crash, three do not

  • X_train, y_train, X_test, y_test: wrong order, ValueError
  • stratify= a number: a screen-long ValueError
  • test_size=25 is 25 rows: no error, a wild score
  • Selecting features before splitting: 0.78 on coin flips
  • A centered 7-day average: $504 instead of $854
WATCH The silent ones are the ones to fear: the code runs and the score looks better.

1.3

Cross-Validation

Five quizzes instead of one exam.

1.3 · Mental model

One exam or five quizzes

  • One exam: a bad night or lucky questions swing the grade
  • Five quizzes on different material: a steadier average, and a spread
  • Each round: one fold validates, the other four train
  • The final exam (the test set) is never used
WHY Cross-validation scores a RECIPE. Afterward, fit the recipe once on all training rows.
1.3 · Code

The fold loop, by hand, once

from sklearn.model_selection import StratifiedKFold
cv = StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=42)
fold_scores = []
for fold, (train_idx, val_idx) in enumerate(cv.split(X_train, y_train), start=1):
    tree = DecisionTreeClassifier(max_depth=4, random_state=42)
    tree.fit(X_train.iloc[train_idx], y_train.iloc[train_idx])
    fold_scores.append(tree.score(X_train.iloc[val_idx], y_train.iloc[val_idx]))
print(np.round(fold_scores, 3), f"mean {np.mean(fold_scores):.3f}")
[0.859 0.866 0.866 0.873 0.85 ] mean 0.863
WHY Positions, so .iloc. A FRESH tree every round.

1.3 · Choose the folds

What will be new to the model?

  • New customers from the same population: StratifiedKFold (shuffled)
  • Next month or season: time-ordered folds, TimeSeriesSplit
  • A new product, store or customer: GroupKFold
  • Groups and class balance: StratifiedGroupKFold

1.3 · Common mistakes

Most of them do not crash

  • Selecting features before cross-validating: 0.825 on pure noise
  • KFold(5, random_state=42) without shuffle=True: ValueError
  • Scorer names: neg_mean_absolute_error, then flip the sign
  • Reporting the best fold instead of the mean
  • Test rows inside the cross-validation
RULE Every step that learns from data goes inside the pipeline you cross-validate.
