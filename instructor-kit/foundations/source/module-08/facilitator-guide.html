Warm-up: Nearest Neighbors and Naive Bayes

10 min
Run it

Put the five questions on the slide. Two minutes alone, one minute with a neighbor, then cold-call gently. While they work, walk the room for the Session 7 homework: the lookalike_shortlist.csv (Lesson 4.1 project) and the review triage files (Lesson 4.2 project).

  • W1 Lesson 4.1: a kNN churn model scores a perfect 1.000 on its training data with k=1. Why, and what do you do about it? Answer: Each training customer is its own nearest neighbor, so k=1 is right by construction (high variance). Choose a larger k by cross-validation; the 4.1 project chose k=51. Ask for the word: overfitting. Today's two models have their own versions: gamma for SVMs, depth for trees.
  • W2 Two unscaled features: spend differs by $300 and days since last order by 120. What share of the squared distance comes from spend? Predict, then check. Answer: 0.86 (86%). 300² = 90,000 against 120² = 14,400: spend takes 86% of the distance purely because of its units. The same reason SVMs need a scaler today.
  • W3 Lesson 4.2: a word appears in 6% of negative reviews and 0.5% of positive ones; 25% of reviews are negative. After seeing the word, about how likely is the review to be negative? Answer: About 0.8: the odds go from 1:3 to (1 × 6) : (3 × 0.5) = 6 : 1.5 = 4 : 1. Prior odds times the likelihood ratio (12). Anyone stuck: draw the 1,000-review table.
  • W4 Why does MultinomialNB need alpha above 0? Answer: Without smoothing a word never seen in one class gives that class a probability of exactly 0, which vetoes all the other evidence. Then bridge: today's SVM on reviews (Lab A) needs no smoothing, but it needs scaling, in its text form (TF-IDF).
  • W5 Your Naive Bayes model ranks customers well (AUC 0.81) but its probabilities pile up near 0 and 1. Can you quote them to the care team? Answer: Rank with it, but recalibrate (CalibratedClassifierCV) before quoting probabilities. Today's SVM gives no probabilities at all, and the same tool fixes it.
Bridge to today

"kNN asked the closest past customers, Naive Bayes multiplied clues. Today: a model that draws the widest possible boundary, and a model that asks yes-or-no questions. By the end of the day you will have five classifiers and an honest way to compare them."

Mental model to say

Point at the board: "Two neighborhoods, red and blue, and a city planner who must build a straight street between them. Many streets would work. The planner picks the widest one, because a wide street leaves room for error: a new house built near either edge still lands on the right side."

Circle the curb houses: "Only these decided where the street went. The houses deep inside could move and nothing would change. They are the support vectors, and they are all the model remembers."

Then the two extensions, one per dial: "Real neighborhoods overlap, so we allow some houses on the street, at a price: that price is C. Real boundaries curve, so we bend the street with a kernel; for the usual RBF kernel, gamma says how far each house's influence reaches."

Live demo (you type, they follow)

New notebook lesson_4_3. Ask for a prediction before every run that prints a number: the street gets narrower as C rises, and gamma's test score collapses while its training score climbs.

  1. The widest street (3m). "Many lines separate these two clouds. The SVM picks the widest street that fits, and only the points on its curbs decide where it goes." Show the lesson's first picture, then run. 
    import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.datasets import make_blobs
from sklearn.svm import SVC

X_toy, y_toy = make_blobs(n_samples=80, centers=[(-2, 0), (2, 0)], cluster_std=1.3, random_state=42)

svm = SVC(kernel="linear", C=1).fit(X_toy, y_toy)
print("support vectors per class:", svm.n_support_)
print("margin width:", round(2 / np.linalg.norm(svm.coef_[0]), 2))
print("training accuracy:", svm.score(X_toy, y_toy))
    support vectors per class: [6 5]
margin width: 1.34
training accuracy: 0.9625
    80 points, 11 support vectors: the other 69 could move freely and nothing would change. Ask: "Why not 100% training accuracy?" (the clouds overlap; no straight line separates them).
  2. C prices the violations (3m). "C is what a point inside the street costs. Cheap violations: a wide street held up by many points. Expensive: a narrow street that bends toward individual points." 
    for C in [0.01, 0.1, 1, 100]:
    model = SVC(kernel="linear", C=C).fit(X_toy, y_toy)
    width = 2 / np.linalg.norm(model.coef_[0])
    print(f"C={C:<6} support vectors {model.n_support_.sum():2d}   margin width {width:.2f}")
    C=0.01   support vectors 42   margin width 4.06
C=0.1    support vectors 18   margin width 2.11
C=1      support vectors 11   margin width 1.34
C=100    support vectors  7   margin width 0.75
    Write on the board: small C = strong regularization = wide street. It is the same C as logistic regression's, the inverse of Lesson 3.2's alpha.
  3. A problem no line can solve (2m). "One class makes a ring around the other. Predict the linear SVM's accuracy." 
    from sklearn.datasets import make_circles
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split

X_ring, y_ring = make_circles(n_samples=300, noise=0.08, factor=0.5, random_state=42)
Xr_train, Xr_test, yr_train, yr_test = train_test_split(X_ring, y_ring, test_size=0.3, stratify=y_ring, random_state=42)

linear_ring = SVC(kernel="linear").fit(Xr_train, yr_train)
print(f"linear SVM, test accuracy {linear_ring.score(Xr_test, yr_test):.3f}")
    linear SVM, test accuracy 0.478
    Worse than a coin flip.
  4. Lift it by hand (3m). "What separates them? Distance from the center. Add it as a third feature and a flat plane in 3-D separates them perfectly." 
    def add_radius(X):
    return np.column_stack([X, (X ** 2).sum(axis=1)])

lifted = SVC(kernel="linear").fit(add_radius(Xr_train), yr_train)
print(f"linear SVM on (x, y, x²+y²), test accuracy {lifted.score(add_radius(Xr_test), yr_test):.3f}")
    linear SVM on (x, y, x²+y²), test accuracy 1.000
    Draw the bowl: inner points low, outer points high, a flat sheet between them. Projected back, the flat sheet is a circle.
  5. Let the kernel do it (3m). "With ten customer features you cannot see which feature to add. The kernel trick never builds the features: it only needs how similar each pair of points is, as if they had been lifted." Write the RBF similarity: exp(-gamma × squared distance). 
    rbf_ring = SVC(kernel="rbf").fit(Xr_train, yr_train)
print(f"RBF SVM, test accuracy {rbf_ring.score(Xr_test, yr_test):.3f}, support vectors {rbf_ring.n_support_.sum()}")
    RBF SVM, test accuracy 1.000, support vectors 42
    Lab B (kernel_by_hand) does exactly this comparison on the churn table: 66 hand-built columns against a kernel that builds none.
  6. gamma is how far each point reaches (4m). "Small gamma: each support vector's influence spreads wide. Huge gamma: a tiny bubble around each point." Ask for a prediction of the test score at gamma=1000 before you run it. 
    from sklearn.datasets import make_moons

X_moon, y_moon = make_moons(n_samples=400, noise=0.3, random_state=42)
Xm_train, Xm_test, ym_train, ym_test = train_test_split(X_moon, y_moon, test_size=0.3, stratify=y_moon, random_state=42)

          f"  support vectors {model.n_support_.sum()}")

  7. Scaling is not optional (3m). "The RBF kernel is distances, and Lesson 4.1 showed what unscaled distances do." Note decision_function in the AUC line: AUC only needs a ranking score. 
    from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score

customers = pd.read_csv("data/customers_churn.csv")
features = ["tenure_months", "orders_12m", "spend_12m", "avg_order_value", "days_since_last_order",
            "returns_12m", "return_rate", "support_tickets_12m", "discount_share", "app_user"]
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(
    customers[features], customers["churned"], test_size=0.25, stratify=customers["churned"], random_state=42
)

for name, model in [("unscaled SVC", SVC()), ("scaled SVC", make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), SVC()))]:
    model.fit(X_train, y_train)
    auc = roc_auc_score(y_test, model.decision_function(X_test))
    print(f"{name:13s} accuracy {model.score(X_test, y_test):.3f}  ROC AUC {auc:.3f}")

scaled SVC    accuracy 0.859  ROC AUC 0.814
    0.767 to 0.814 from one line. The scaler goes INSIDE the pipeline (Lesson 2.2) so cross-validation never peeks.
  8. A fair comparison on churn (4m). "Baseline, logistic regression, an RBF SVM, and the same SVM told that a missed churner costs more."

Three mistakes to expect, and how to coach each
  • Judging an SVM by accuracy on imbalanced data. The linear SVM that predicts nobody churns scores 0.835, exactly the baseline. Coach: "How many churners did it flag?" Then AUC, recall, or class_weight.
  • Tuning by the training score. gamma=10 on churn: training 0.996, test AUC 0.699, 4,491 of 4,500 customers as support vectors. Coach: "What share of the customers are support vectors? What does that say?" Choose C and gamma by CV.
  • Probabilities from an SVM. predict_proba raises; probability=True warns. Coach: "Do you need a probability or a ranking?" Ranking: decision_function. Probability: calibrate.

Checks for understanding

Ask these during the demo and the labs:

  • You delete a training point far from the boundary and refit. What changes? Answer: nothing: it was not a support vector.
  • Which way do you move C to get a simpler boundary? Answer: down: cheaper violations, wider street.
  • Why does roc_auc_score accept decision_function output? Answer: AUC only needs scores that rank; the signed distance ranks.
  • When would you NOT use a kernel SVM? Answer: many rows (training time grows roughly with the square of the rows): use LinearSVC, logistic regression or trees.

Mental model to say

Draw on the trail map: "At the trailhead a signpost asks: has it been more than about 170 days since your last order? Each answer sends the customer down a different trail, to the next signpost. Every trail ends at a campsite with a sign: the share of past customers who walked that way and then left."

"Training a tree means choosing which question to post at each junction: it tries every feature and every threshold and keeps the one that makes the two piles purest. Predicting means walking a new customer down the trails and reading the campsite's sign." Write Gini = chance that two customers picked from a node disagree next to the map.

  2. Gini by hand (3m). "Pick two customers from a node at random; Gini is the chance they disagree. A good split makes purer children." 
    def gini(counts):
    p = np.asarray(counts) / np.sum(counts)
    return 1 - np.sum(p ** 2)

root = np.bincount(y_train)                                        # [stayed, churned]
left = np.bincount(y_train[X_train["days_since_last_order"] <= 172.5])
right = root - left
weighted = (left.sum() * gini(left) + right.sum() * gini(right)) / root.sum()

print("root", root, " left", left, " right", right)
print(f"Gini root {gini(root):.4f}  left {gini(left):.4f}  right {gini(right):.4f}")
print(f"weighted Gini of the children {weighted:.4f}, decrease {gini(root) - weighted:.4f}")

t = small_tree.tree_

Three mistakes to expect, and how to coach each
  • Reporting the training score. An unlimited tree: training 1.000, test 0.822, baseline 0.835. Coach: "Which customers was that 1.000 measured on?"
  • Ranking with an unpruned tree. Its probabilities are all 0 or 1 (AUC 0.683): you cannot choose a top 50 from ties. Coach: "How many distinct probabilities does it give?" (np.unique). Fix: min_samples_leaf, or a forest next session.
  • Reading importance as truth. spend_12m 0.012, yet churn falls from 24% to 8% across spend quartiles. Coach: "Did the model need it, or does it not matter?"

Checks for understanding

Ask these during the demo and the labs:

  • Why do the two second-level questions differ? Answer: the search repeats inside each child, using only that child's customers.
  • A depth-2 tree gives how many distinct probabilities? Answer: at most four, one per leaf.
  • Does a tree need scaled features? Answer: no: scaling moves thresholds, not customers.
  • Which tells you the right tree size: training score, test score, or CV on the training set? Answer: CV on the training set; the test set once at the end.

  1. Your SVM scores 0.996 on the training customers and AUC 0.699 on the test set, with 4,491 of 4,500 customers as support vectors. Which dial is set wrong, and how do you choose it properly? Answer: gamma is far too large (each customer's influence is a tiny bubble, so the model memorized the training set; nearly every point is a support vector). Choose C and gamma together with cross-validation on the training set (keys svc__C, svc__gamma), never with the training score.
  2. A node holds 40 stayers and 10 churners. What is its Gini impurity, and what churn probability does a tree give a customer who lands there? Answer: Gini = 1 - (0.8² + 0.2²) = 0.32. The probability is the leaf's churn share: 10 / 50 = 0.2.
