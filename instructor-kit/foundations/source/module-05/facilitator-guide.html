Session 5: Feature Selection and Imbalanced Classes

How to teach the fifth session of Machine Learning Foundations: feature selection (filters, wrappers, L1, and the tolerance rule that cuts Pinecrest's churn model from 16 inputs to 6) and imbalanced classes (why accuracy fails on 0.9% fraud, what class weights and samplers really do, and how to set a threshold from the price of each mistake). Both lessons share one warning: a step that looks at the labels must happen inside each training fold, or cross-validation reports 80% on pure noise and AP 0.91 on fiction.

Questions and answers
  • W1. A pipeline is make_pipeline(SimpleImputer(), StandardScaler(), LogisticRegression()). What does pipe.predict(X_new) do to each of the three steps? Answer: The imputer and the scaler only transform the new rows, with the medians, means and standard deviations they learned during fit; the model then predicts. Nothing is refitted. If someone says "it refits the scaler", ask what would happen with one new customer: a scaler fitted on one row.
  • W2. In Lesson 2.2 the signup-date churn rate scored CV ROC AUC 0.930 when computed with pandas, and 0.586 as a TargetEncoder inside the pipeline. Which is honest, and why was the other so high? Answer: 0.586. The pandas rates used every training row's label, including the rows that later sat in each validation fold; with one or two customers per date, "the rate on your date" was mostly your own label. This is today's selection leak in another costume: any step that looks at y belongs inside the folds.
  • W3. What does this print? (code on the slide) Answer: ['logisticregression__C'] make_pipeline names each step after its class in lowercase; step__parameter reaches any setting. Today you swap a whole step the same way: set_params(logisticregression=...).
  • W4. add_churn_features builds tickets_no_app = support_tickets_12m * (1 - app_user). What is it for an app user with 3 tickets, and why build it? Answer: 0. Tickets raise churn for customers without the app but not for app users; a linear model cannot express that from the two raw columns, so the product gives non-app tickets their own weight. Keep this pair in mind: in today's demo forward selection keeps both raw columns because they matter together.
  • W5. Why can FunctionTransformer(add_churn_features) be fitted outside the folds without leaking, while TargetEncoder cannot? Answer: The feature function is stateless: its thresholds are fixed numbers and it learns nothing from the rows it sees. TargetEncoder learns rates from y, so fitting it on validation rows hands them their own answers. Feature selection and resampling, today's two subjects, are both "learn from y" steps.

Mental model to say

"Experienced hikers weigh every item before a long trip. Two stoves? One goes home: a redundant feature. The camp chair you never sit in? Home: an irrelevant feature. But the water filter stays, even though you use it twice a day. The goal is not the lightest pack; it is the lightest pack that still gets you to the end of the trail. In model terms: the smallest feature set that keeps the score."

Then the honest promise, in one breath: "Selection rarely makes a good model much more accurate. It makes it simpler to collect, explain and monitor. On Pinecrest's churn table it will cut 16 inputs to 6 at no cost." Draw three columns on the board, Filter · Wrapper · Embedded, with one line each: scores columns alone (fast, blind to the model and to teamwork) · tries subsets with the real model (slow, sees teamwork) · selects while training (one fit, tied to one model type).

Close with the three habits, written under the columns and left there: decide with cross-validation · keep selection inside the pipeline · prefer the smallest set within a tolerance of the best.

  5. A filter inside the pipeline (3m). "SelectKBest keeps the k best; in a pipeline it is refitted on every training fold. Predict: does picking the best six beat using all eleven?" 
    from functools import partial
from sklearn.feature_selection import SelectKBest
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.model_selection import StratifiedKFold, cross_val_score
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler

cv = StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=42)
mi = partial(mutual_info_classif, random_state=42)

def churn_pipe(*steps):
    return make_pipeline(SimpleImputer(strategy="median"), StandardScaler(), *steps, LogisticRegression())

for name, pipe in [("all 11 columns", churn_pipe()),
                   ("best 6 by F", churn_pipe(SelectKBest(f_classif, k=6))),
                   ("best 6 by MI", churn_pipe(SelectKBest(mi, k=6)))]:
    scores = cross_val_score(pipe, X_train, y_train, cv=cv, scoring="roc_auc")
    print(f"{name:15s} CV ROC AUC {scores.mean():.3f}")

  9. The most important experiment: pure noise (4m). "200 rows, 5,000 random columns, a random target. Honest accuracy is a coin flip. Watch what selecting first, then cross-validating, reports." 
    def noise_experiment(seed):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    X_noise = rng.normal(size=(200, 5000))
    y_noise = rng.integers(0, 2, size=200)

Three mistakes to expect, and how to coach each
  • Selecting before the split or outside the pipeline. Symptom: a CV score that rises when they select (often with SelectKBest(...).fit_transform(X_train, y_train) and then cross_val_score on the result). Coach: "Which rows did the selector see? Did the validation folds help choose the columns?" Point at the noise experiment's two numbers.
  • L1 or RFE on unscaled columns, or the default solver. Symptoms: ValueError: Solver lbfgs supports only 'l2' or None penalties, got l1 penalty., or an L1 model that keeps avg_order_value and drops discount_share. Coach: "What does the penalty charge for? Is a weight of 3 on a 0-to-1 column the same as 3 on dollars?"
  • Reading the winner instead of the curve, and "dropped" as "irrelevant". Symptoms: "RFECV says keep all 11", or "L1 shows average order value doesn't matter". Coach: "How far apart are the scores on the plateau, and how far apart are the folds?" and "Dropped given what else is in the model?"

Mental model to say

"A smoke detector that never beeps is right 364 days a year, and worthless. One that beeps every time you make toast catches every fire, and soon gets its battery pulled. A useful detector sits between the two, and where exactly depends on what a false alarm costs compared with a missed fire."

Then split the board in two: RANK (does the model put frauds near the top? average precision against the base rate) and DECIDE (where do we cut? cost, capacity or a recall target). "Today's big surprise: class weights and resampling, the fixes every tutorial reaches for, mostly change DECIDE, not RANK, and you can change DECIDE directly by moving the threshold." Write cost = $10 × flagged + $375 × missed under DECIDE.

    from sklearn.compose import ColumnTransformer

  4. Then the decisions at 0.5 (2m). "A helper that prints what a fraud team cares about." 
    from sklearn.metrics import confusion_matrix

def report(name, y_true, y_pred):
    tn, fp, fn, tp = confusion_matrix(y_true, y_pred, labels=[0, 1]).ravel()
    print(f"{name:16s} flagged {tp + fp:5d}  caught {tp:3d}  missed {fn:3d}  "
          f"recall {tp / (tp + fn):.2f}  precision {tp / max(tp + fp, 1):.2f}")

  7. Three samplers, inside an imblearn pipeline (4m). "Samplers change the training rows. imblearn's pipeline runs them during fit and skips them during predict, so test rows keep the real balance." Start the cell, then talk through the three samplers while it runs. 
    from imblearn.under_sampling import RandomUnderSampler

  8. Price the mistakes (3m). "A review costs $10 of analyst time; a missed fraud costs about $375. Cost = $10 per flag plus $375 per miss." 
    REVIEW_COST, FRAUD_LOSS = 10, 375

def fraud_cost(y_true, y_pred):
    tn, fp, fn, tp = confusion_matrix(y_true, y_pred, labels=[0, 1]).ravel()
    return int(REVIEW_COST * (tp + fp) + FRAUD_LOSS * fn)

print(f"review nothing   : ${fraud_cost(y_test, np.zeros(len(y_test), dtype=int)):,}")
print(f"review everything: ${fraud_cost(y_test, np.ones(len(y_test), dtype=int)):,}")
for threshold in [0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01]:
    pred = (p_plain >= threshold).astype(int)
    print(f"threshold {threshold:<5} cost ${fraud_cost(y_test, pred):,}")

threshold 0.02  cost $27,250
threshold 0.01  cost $33,230

  9. The formula, and why not to pick on the test set (3m). "Flag when p × $375 > $10: p > 10/375. That works because the plain model's probabilities are honest." 
    print(f"formula threshold: {REVIEW_COST} / {FRAUD_LOSS} = {REVIEW_COST / FRAUD_LOSS:.3f}")
thresholds = np.geomspace(0.003, 0.9, 80)

  10. TunedThresholdClassifierCV (3m). "Cross-validation tries many thresholds on the training folds, keeps the cheapest, then refits."

  11. The leak, on purpose (3m). "Resample first, then cross-validate. Predict the AP."

Three mistakes to expect, and how to coach each
  • A sampler in a scikit-learn pipeline, or resampling before cross-validation. Symptoms: TypeError: All intermediate steps should be transformers and implement fit and transform or be the string 'passthrough...; or a CV AP near 0.9. Coach: "Which pipeline did you import? Did the validation folds keep the real fraud rate?"
  • Judging by accuracy or by recall at 0.5. Symptoms: "the balanced model is better, recall 0.69"; cross_val_score without scoring=. Coach: "How many payments did it flag to get that recall? What did AP do?"
  • The formula threshold on weighted or resampled probabilities, or a threshold chosen on the test set. Symptoms: a model that flags everything; a "best threshold" read off the test-set cost curve. Coach: "Are these probabilities honest? Compare their mean with the real rate." and "Which rows chose the threshold?"
