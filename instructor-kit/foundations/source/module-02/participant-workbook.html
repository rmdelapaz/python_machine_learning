1.2

Splits, Baselines and Leakage

By the end you can
  • Measure how much a test score moves from one random split to another, and explain why stratifying helps
  • Make a train / validation / test split and use each part for its one job
  • Split by time and by group when rows are related, and show how much a careless random split flatters a model
  • Pick a baseline that fits the problem (most frequent class, mean, yesterday, same day last week)
  • Detect target leakage and train-test contamination, and remove them
Key ideas
  • The dress rehearsal rule. Evaluate the model under the same conditions it will face in real use. Every way of breaking the rule makes the score look better, which is why nobody notices.
  • One split is one sample. Over 30 seeds the same model scored 0.836 to 0.866 (standard deviation about 0.007). Differences smaller than the wobble mean nothing.
  • Stratify classification splits on the target (stratify=y), so the class share and with it the baseline stay fixed. Never stratify on a feature or a number.
  • Train fits, validation chooses, test grades once. Call train_test_split twice for 60/20/20 (test_size=0.20, then 0.25 of the rest). After the one look at the test set, no more choices.
  • Time-ordered data needs a time split (train on the past, test on the future): on daily sales a random split claimed $613 a day, the truth was $854.
  • Related rows need a group split when the model will meet new groups: GroupShuffleSplit(..., groups=...). New products: 17.84 units off instead of 10.23.
  • Beat the strongest free baseline: most frequent class, the mean, yesterday, the same day last week ($1,072 for daily sales).
  • Leakage: target leakage (recorded after the outcome), train-test contamination (a step fitted before the split), time leakage (the future helps), group leakage. Catch it with a single-feature audit and "known at prediction time?".
Worked example: the same customer on both sides?

Pinecrest's recommendation team wants to know how well a model will do for customers it has never seen. interactions.csv has one row per customer and product, so most customers have many rows. Does a random split give the model strangers to predict?

import pandas as pd
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split, GroupShuffleSplit
inter = pd.read_csv("data/interactions.csv")
print(inter.shape, inter["customer_id"].nunique(), "customers")
row_train, row_test = train_test_split(inter, test_size=0.25, random_state=42)
shared = set(row_train["customer_id"]) & set(row_test["customer_id"])
print(f"random rows: {row_test['customer_id'].nunique()} test customers, {len(shared)} also in training")
(32178, 5) 3000 customers
random rows: 2649 test customers, 2641 also in training

Almost every test customer (2,641 of 2,649) also has rows in training. A model graded this way is graded on customers it already knows: an easier exam than "new customers". Now split by customer instead.

gss = GroupShuffleSplit(n_splits=1, test_size=0.25, random_state=42)
train_idx, test_idx = next(gss.split(inter, groups=inter["customer_id"]))
g_train, g_test = inter.iloc[train_idx], inter.iloc[test_idx]
shared = set(g_train["customer_id"]) & set(g_test["customer_id"])
print(f"group split: {len(g_test):,} test rows, {g_test['customer_id'].nunique()} test customers, "
      f"{len(shared)} also in training")
group split: 7,999 test rows, 750 test customers, 0 also in training

A quarter of the customers (750) and all their rows go to the test side; none of them appears in training. Which split is right depends on the question: "recommend more products to our existing customers" is a row (or time) split; "how well will this work for new sign-ups?" is a group split. Choosing the split is choosing the question.

1.3

Cross-Validation

By the end you can
  • Explain why one train/validation split gives a noisy score, and how k-fold cross-validation averages that noise away
  • Run cross_val_score and cross_validate with a stratified, shuffled splitter, and report a mean with its spread
  • Keep preprocessing inside the folds by cross-validating a make_pipeline instead of pre-scaled data
  • Compare two models fold by fold on identical folds, and get out-of-fold predictions with cross_val_predict
  • Choose TimeSeriesSplit or GroupKFold when rows are related by time or by group
Key ideas
  • One exam or five quizzes. K-fold cross-validation trains k times, validates every training row exactly once, and gives k scores: report the mean plus or minus the spread.
  • It scores a recipe, not a model. cross_val_score fits fresh copies and throws them away; afterward fit the chosen recipe once on all the training rows.
  • Build the splitter yourself: StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=42) for classification, KFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=42) for regression. cv=5 does not shuffle.
  • Preprocessing goes inside the folds: cross-validate make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), LogisticRegression()), never pre-scaled data. Selecting features outside the folds turned pure noise into 0.825 accuracy.
  • cross_validate gives several metrics and, with return_train_score=True, the training score: a big train-validation gap means overfitting.
  • Compare on identical folds and look at paired differences. A lead smaller than the fold-to-fold spread is a tie (12 of 15 folds, +0.004: a tie). Then prefer the simpler model.
  • cross_val_predict gives every training row an honest out-of-fold prediction, for charts and thresholds.
  • Match the folds to the question: what will be new to the model? New customers: shuffled stratified folds. Next period: time-ordered folds. A new product or store: GroupKFold.
Worked example: recency alone, five quizzes

How much does recency alone tell you about churn, honestly? Cross-validate a two-level tree on ONE feature against the know-nothing baseline, with training scores next to the validation scores.

import pandas as pd
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split, StratifiedKFold, cross_validate
from sklearn.tree import DecisionTreeClassifier
from sklearn.dummy import DummyClassifier
customers = pd.read_csv("data/customers_churn.csv")
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(
    customers[["days_since_last_order"]], customers["churned"],
    test_size=0.25, stratify=customers["churned"], random_state=42)
cv = StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=42)
for name, model in [("baseline", DummyClassifier(strategy="prior")),
                    ("recency tree", DecisionTreeClassifier(max_depth=2, random_state=42))]:
    r = cross_validate(model, X_train, y_train, cv=cv, scoring="roc_auc", return_train_score=True)
    print(f"{name:12s} AUC {r['test_score'].mean():.3f} +/- {r['test_score'].std():.3f}"
          f"  (train {r['train_score'].mean():.3f})")
baseline     AUC 0.500 +/- 0.000  (train 0.500)
recency tree AUC 0.703 +/- 0.023  (train 0.709)

Ranking AUC 0.703 against 0.500 for the baseline: recency alone carries real signal. Train and validation are close (0.709 vs 0.703), so the small tree is not memorizing. Now look inside the five rounds: cross-validation fits five different models.

for fold, (tr, va) in enumerate(cv.split(X_train, y_train), start=1):
    tree = DecisionTreeClassifier(max_depth=2, random_state=42).fit(X_train.iloc[tr], y_train.iloc[tr])
    print(f"fold {fold}: first question 'days_since_last_order <= {tree.tree_.threshold[0]:.1f}?'")
fold 1: first question 'days_since_last_order <= 172.5?'
fold 2: first question 'days_since_last_order <= 172.5?'
fold 3: first question 'days_since_last_order <= 164.5?'
fold 4: first question 'days_since_last_order <= 172.0?'
fold 5: first question 'days_since_last_order <= 158.5?'

Five fold models, five slightly different first questions, all near 160 to 170 days (the churn risk jumps for customers who have not ordered in about five months). That is why cross-validation scores a recipe ("a depth-2 tree on recency"), not one model: afterward you fit the recipe once on all the training rows.

Glossary

TermMeaning
wobble (split noise)How much a score moves when only the random split changes
stratifyKeep the class share the same in every part of a split (stratify=y)
validation setData used to compare candidates and choose one; it may influence your choices
test setData locked away until the choice is made, then used once to report the result
time splitTrain on earlier rows, test on later ones, as in real use
group splitEvery row of the same entity (product, customer, store) on one side
naive / seasonal naiveForecast baselines: tomorrow = today; next Saturday = last Saturday
MAEMean absolute error: the average size of a miss, in the target's units
data leakageInformation during training or testing that the model will not have in real use
target leakageA feature recorded after, or because of, the outcome (cancellation_reason)
train-test contaminationTest or validation rows influence training, e.g. a scaler fitted before the split
k-fold cross-validationTrain k times, each time validating on a different fold; report mean and spread
foldOne of the k equal parts the training rows are cut into
pipelineSteps chained into one estimator (make_pipeline), refitted together in every fold
out-of-fold predictionA prediction for a row from the fold model that did not train on it (cross_val_predict)
ROC AUCHow well a model ranks positives above negatives: 0.5 random, 1.0 perfect (Lesson 1.4)
