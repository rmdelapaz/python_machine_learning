Session 2: Splits, Leakage and Cross-Validation

How to teach the second session of Machine Learning Foundations: make a score honest. Lesson 1.2 measures how much a single split wobbles, gives every part of a three-way split one job, splits by time and by group when rows are related, picks the strongest free baseline and catches a leak with a single-feature audit. Lesson 1.3 replaces the single split with cross-validation: stratified shuffled folds, preprocessing inside a pipeline, paired comparisons, out-of-fold predictions, and folds that match the question. Learners leave knowing that every careless evaluation looks better than the honest one: 0.941 with a leak against 0.848, $613 on a random split against $854, and 0.825 accuracy on pure noise.

2 lessons 3 hrs session Lessons 1.2–1.3 Session 2 of 11

How to Use This Guide

  • Same rhythm for both lessons: frame (one mental model) → live demo (you type, they type along) → guided lab (a kit lab, new practice on the lesson's skills) → start the lesson's own project. The lesson exercises, quiz and journal are homework.
  • Type, do not paste. Every code block in the demo scripts below was run to produce the output printed under it (Python 3.12, pandas 3.0, scikit-learn 1.9 on 4 CPU threads); the code is the lessons' own, so learners' pages show the same numbers. Ask for a prediction before every run that prints a score: this session is built on scores that look better than they are, and a surprise only teaches if the learner committed to a guess.
  • The lessons are the reference. Keep evaluation_train_test_split.html and evaluation_cross_validation.html open for the pictures (the 30-split histogram, the 60/20/20 bar, the Boise time-split chart, the leak flowchart, the 5-fold grid, the paired strip plot). Labs live in labs/module-02/: _starter.py files are handouts, _solution.py files are yours.
  • This session is one idea told twice: a score is only as honest as the data it was graded on. Lesson 1.2 does it with single splits (stratify, a validation set, time and group splits, leaks); Lesson 1.3 replaces the single split with folds. Say the bridge out loud at the break: "Before the break we measured the wobble; after it we average it away."
  • Teal blocks are hands-on, amber blocks are watch-outs. Lesson codes (1.2, 1.3) match the course site.
The #1 risk of Session 2: a room that hears "the score went up" as good news

Every careless evaluation in these two lessons looks BETTER than the honest one: 0.941 with the leak, $613 on a random split, 0.825 on pure noise, the best fold instead of the mean. Learners who are used to "higher is better" will cheer each time. Make the reverse reflex the session's habit: when a number jumps, the first question is "what does this column, this split or this step know that it would not know in real use?" The second risk is time: both demos are long and both lessons end in a 40-minute project. Protect the demo steps that change minds (30 seeds, the time split, the leak audit; the paired comparison and the three splitters) and cut the mechanics first (see If You Run Long).

Five retrieval questions, with answers
  1. W1 On the churn table, what does always predicting "stays" score? And "never fraud" on the payments? Answer: 0.835 on churn (16.5% churn) and 0.9912 on fraud (0.88% fraud). Every score today is read against a do-nothing baseline.
  2. W2 Which column of customers_churn.csv must never be a feature, and why? Answer: cancellation_reason: it is filled in only after a customer has left, so on prediction day it is always empty. Today Lesson 1.2 measures what it does to a model (0.848 becomes 0.941).
  3. W3 train_test_split(X, y, ...) returns four things. In which order? Answer: X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test: the X parts, then the y parts. Today's Common Mistake 1 is the wrong order.
  4. W4 Why is the scaler fitted on the training rows only, and then used to transform both sets? Answer: The test rows stand in for future customers, whose numbers could not have been seen when the model was built. Today the same rule moves inside every cross-validation fold.
  5. W5 Homework: what did DummyClassifier(strategy="constant", constant=1) score on the 1.1 test set? Answer: 0.165 (0.165): it calls everyone a churner, so it is right exactly for the 16.5% who churn. Accuracy rewards the majority class.

Splits, Baselines and Leakage

75 min
Objectives (from the lesson page)
  • Measure how much a test score moves from one random split to another, and explain why stratifying helps
  • Make a train / validation / test split and use each part for its one job
  • Split by time and by group when rows are related, and show how much a careless random split flatters a model

  • Detect target leakage and train-test contamination, and remove them

Mental model to say

Say: "A theater company rehearses for weeks, then holds one dress rehearsal run exactly like opening night: full costumes, no stopping, no script. If the dress rehearsal is easier than opening night, a prompter whispering lines or half the audience missing, it tells you nothing about the real show."

Then the rule, written on the board and left there: evaluate the model under the same conditions it will face in real use. "If it will predict next month from data up to this month, the test must be later than the training data. If it will score brand-new products, the test must hold products it has never seen. If a column will not exist at prediction time, the model must not see it in testing either."

Then the warning that makes the lesson stick: "Every section today breaks the rule in a different way, and every time, the careless evaluation looks BETTER. Nobody goes looking for a bug when the score goes up. That is why these mistakes survive."

  2. One split is one sample (3m). "In 1.1 logistic regression scored 0.848 on the split with seed 42. Forty-two was arbitrary. Let's ask thirty other seeds." Before running: "Write down the lowest and highest score you expect." 
    def split_and_score(seed, test_size=0.25):
    X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(
        X, y, test_size=test_size, stratify=y, random_state=seed
    )
    scaler = StandardScaler().fit(X_train)
    model = LogisticRegression().fit(scaler.transform(X_train), y_train)
    return model.score(scaler.transform(X_test), y_test)

scores = pd.Series([split_and_score(seed) for seed in range(30)])
print(scores.describe().round(3))

  3. Stratify, or the yardstick moves too (2m). "Without stratify=y the churn share in the test set wobbles as well, and the baseline is one minus that share." 
    test_rates = {"random": [], "stratified": []}

Checks for understanding

Cross-Validation

75 min
Objectives (from the lesson page)
  • Explain why one train/validation split gives a noisy score, and how k-fold cross-validation averages that noise away
  • Run cross_val_score and cross_validate with a stratified, shuffled splitter, and report a mean with its spread
  • Keep preprocessing inside the folds by cross-validating a make_pipeline instead of pre-scaled data
  • Compare two models fold by fold on identical folds, and get out-of-fold predictions with cross_val_predict

Mental model to say

Say: "A teacher who grades a student on one exam learns something, but a bad night's sleep or a lucky choice of questions can swing it. Five shorter quizzes, each on different material, give a steadier picture, and the spread of the five grades tells the teacher how much to trust the average. Cross-validation gives your model five quizzes instead of one exam, and it does so without using the final exam at all."

Point at the wobble you wrote on the board before the break (about 0.007 for a test set, about two points between ten splits): "Before the break we measured the noise. Now we average it away, and report its size." Then the two sentences that prevent the most confusion: "Cross-validation evaluates a recipe, not a model: it fits five models and throws them away. When you like the recipe, you fit it once on all the training rows." And: "In scikit-learn's names, 'test' inside cross-validation means the validation fold. Our real test set stays locked."

  • Step 3 on their own: one RepeatedStratifiedKFold, two metrics, all four candidates. It fits 60 models (the forest dominates: 10 to 40 seconds on a laptop). Done = the leaderboard with random forest on top (accuracy 0.871, ROC AUC 0.867 ± 0.018) and the baseline at 0.835 / 0.500.

Checks for understanding

Ask these out loud during the demo and the lab:

  • How many models does cross_val_score(tree, X_train, y_train, cv=5) fit, and which one do you keep? Answer: five; none. You refit the recipe once on all of X_train.
  • Why build StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=42) instead of passing cv=5? Answer: cv=5 does not shuffle; on rows sorted in any meaningful order the folds are distorted (0% and 83% churners).
  • Why does the scaler go into make_pipeline? Answer: so it is refitted on the training folds of every round and never sees the validation fold.
  • The tree beats logistic regression in 12 of 15 folds by 0.004 AUC. Verdict? Answer: a tie: the lead is far smaller than the fold-to-fold spread (about 0.02). Prefer the simpler or cheaper model.
  • A model will forecast next quarter. What will be new to it, and which folds match? Answer: the future; time-ordered folds (train on the past, validate on the period right after).

Closing

Exit ticket (3 questions, no notes, 4 minutes)

Put the three questions on the slide. Learners answer in the workbook's Exit Ticket box or on a sticky note. Sort the notes into "got it" and "not yet" before Session 3: question 3 tells you whether the Session 3 warm-up needs a minute on pipelines inside folds before Lesson 1.4 uses cross_val_predict on a pipeline.

  1. Name the three parts of a three-way split and the one job of each. Which part is opened only once? Answer: Training fits every candidate; validation compares candidates and chooses; test grades the final choice, opened once at the very end (or cross-validation on the training rows replaces the fixed validation set).
  2. A sales forecast scores MAE $613 on a random split and $854 when trained on 2023 and 2024 and tested on 2025. Which number do you report, and why is the other one lower? Answer: $854, the time split: it is how the forecast will be used (trained on the past, asked about the future). The random split is lower because almost every test day has its neighbors, the day before and the day after, in training, and sales have momentum; tomorrow's neighbors are never available.
  3. Why do you cross-validate make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), LogisticRegression()) instead of scaling X_train first and cross-validating the model? Answer: So the scaler is refitted inside every round on the training folds only. Pre-scaling lets every validation fold help compute the mean and standard deviation it is scaled with: a leak, tiny for a scaler and large for steps that learn more (selecting features, encoding categories).
