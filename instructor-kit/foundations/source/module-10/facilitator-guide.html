Warm-up

Warm-up: Bagging and Boosting

10 min
Five retrieval questions (no notes; answers after each)
  1. You draw a bootstrap sample of 10,000 rows from 10,000. About how many distinct rows does it hold, and what are the others called? Answer: About 6,300 (63.2%); the rest are out-of-bag, and a forest's OOB score is computed from them.
  2. A forest with 300 trees has an OOB ROC AUC of 0.866. What happens if you raise n_estimators to 3,000? Answer: About the same AUC, ten times the fitting time. More trees never overfit; the score plateaus.
  3. In gradient boosting, what is each new tree trained to predict? Answer: What the current model still gets wrong: the residuals (more generally the negative gradient of the loss).
  4. You early-stop an XGBoost or HistGradientBoosting model. Which rows may decide when it stops? Answer: A validation set split off from the training rows, never the test set.
  5. HistGradientBoostingClassifier on churn needs which preparation for the five text columns? Answer: Only astype("category"): no one-hot encoding, no imputer, no scaler (missing values stay as they are).
Homework check
  • Lesson 5.3 "Before the Next Lesson" asked: will averaging a logistic regression's and the booster's probabilities beat the booster alone? Take a show of hands, write the split on the board, and leave it: demo Step 4 answers it (0.858 against 0.862: no).
  • Lesson 5.3's other prediction: rerunning the early-stopping block with random_state=7 moves the number of trees and the test AUC. Ask who saw how much. That wobble is the reason for Lesson 6.1.
  • Projects: the 5.2 call list (the forest reached 41 churners in its top 50) and the 5.3 demand forecaster (Poisson boosting MAE 8.05 against 13.42 for the per-product mean). Anyone who did not finish 5.3 should do so this week: Final Project B builds on it.

Voting and Stacking

65 min
Objectives (from the lesson page)
  • Explain why a group of models beats its members only when their errors differ, and measure that diversity
  • Build hard and soft VotingClassifier ensembles from complete preprocessing pipelines

Mental model to say

"Pinecrest asks a panel of three testers whether a new jacket is waterproof enough to stock. If each is right 70% of the time and they judge independently, the majority is right more often than any one of them: their mistakes rarely line up. If all three read the same online review and repeat it, the panel is no better than the review. A crowd is wise only when its members make different mistakes."

Then the two ways of combining: "Voting is the panel counting hands. Stacking is a manager who has watched the panel for a season and learned whose opinion to trust, and how much. The manager must learn from the panel's real track record, not from what they say about jackets they have already tested: that is the out-of-fold rule."

And the professional's question, written on the board for the whole lesson: is the gain larger than the noise, and is it worth the moving parts?

Live demo (you type, they follow)

New notebook lesson_5_4. Steps 2, 3 and 8 fit models: start them, then talk. Ask for a prediction before Steps 1, 4 and 10.

  1. Simulate the panel (3m). "Each voter is right 70% of the time. Watch what happens as more voters join, and as they start copying one shared opinion." Ask first: five independent 70% voters, majority vote: better or worse than one? 
    import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(42)

def crowd_accuracy(n_voters, shared, p_correct=0.7, n_cases=20_000):
    common = rng.random(n_cases) < p_correct                  # the shared opinion
    own = rng.random((n_cases, n_voters)) < p_correct         # each voter's own judgment
    copies = rng.random((n_cases, n_voters)) < shared         # who copies the shared opinion
    correct = np.where(copies, common[:, None], own)
    return (correct.sum(axis=1) > n_voters / 2).mean()        # majority vote right?

for shared in [0.0, 0.5, 0.9]:
    accs = [crowd_accuracy(n, shared) for n in (1, 5, 25)]
    print(f"shared {shared:.1f}:  1 voter {accs[0]:.3f}   5 voters {accs[1]:.3f}   25 voters {accs[2]:.3f}")
    shared 0.0:  1 voter 0.695   5 voters 0.837   25 voters 0.982
shared 0.5:  1 voter 0.704   5 voters 0.754   25 voters 0.721
shared 0.9:  1 voter 0.703   5 voters 0.699   25 voters 0.702
    Independent: 0.695 → 0.837 → 0.982. Half copying: barely better. 90% copying: no better than one voter. Say: "The question for any ensemble is how different their mistakes still are."
  2. Three members that see the data differently (3m). "Each member is a complete pipeline, so it brings its own preprocessing and nothing can leak inside cross-validation." Narrate the three: a straight line, deep averaged trees, small trees in sequence. 
    import pandas as pd
from sklearn.compose import make_column_transformer
from sklearn.ensemble import HistGradientBoostingClassifier, RandomForestClassifier
from sklearn.impute import SimpleImputer
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.metrics import average_precision_score, roc_auc_score
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import OneHotEncoder, StandardScaler

customers = pd.read_csv("data/customers_churn.csv")
numeric = ["tenure_months", "orders_12m", "spend_12m", "avg_order_value", "days_since_last_order",
           "returns_12m", "return_rate", "support_tickets_12m", "discount_share", "app_user",
           "email_opens_90d"]
categorical = ["home_store", "region", "age_group", "loyalty_tier", "preferred_category"]

X = customers[numeric + categorical].copy()
X[categorical] = X[categorical].astype("category")
y = customers["churned"]
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(X, y, test_size=0.25, stratify=y, random_state=42)

impute_encode = make_pipeline(SimpleImputer(strategy="most_frequent"),
                              OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore", sparse_output=False))
logreg = make_pipeline(
    make_column_transformer((make_pipeline(SimpleImputer(strategy="median"), StandardScaler()), numeric),
                            (impute_encode, categorical)),
    LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000),
)
forest = make_pipeline(
    make_column_transformer((SimpleImputer(strategy="median"), numeric), (impute_encode, categorical)),
    RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=200, min_samples_leaf=5, random_state=42, n_jobs=-1),
)
boost = HistGradientBoostingClassifier(random_state=42)

members = [("logreg", logreg), ("forest", forest), ("boost", boost)]
test_proba = pd.DataFrame(index=X_test.index)
for name, model in members:
    model.fit(X_train, y_train)
    test_proba[name] = model.predict_proba(X_test)[:, 1]
    print(f"{name:7s} ROC AUC {roc_auc_score(y_test, test_proba[name]):.3f}"
          f"  AP {average_precision_score(y_test, test_proba[name]):.3f}")
    logreg  ROC AUC 0.826  AP 0.493
forest  ROC AUC 0.861  AP 0.606
boost   ROC AUC 0.862  AP 0.609
    The two tree models are close; logistic regression is clearly weaker (0.826).
  3. How different are their mistakes? (3m). "Measuring that on the test set would be peeking. Out-of-fold predictions: every training customer gets a prediction from a model that never saw them."

Three mistakes to expect, and how to coach each
  • Measuring diversity or choosing weights on the test set. Symptom: test_proba.corr() or a loop over weights scored with y_test. Coach: "Which rows decided this choice? If the test set chose it, what is left to grade it?" Out-of-fold predictions on the training rows are the honest place for every ensemble decision.
  • Training the meta-model on in-sample predictions. No error, a plausible number (0.854), wrong weights. Coach with the in-sample vs out-of-fold table: "Would you trust a tester's opinion of jackets they designed themselves?" Fix: cross_val_predict or StackingClassifier(cv=...).
  • Shipping a third-decimal win. "The stack wins, 0.864 against 0.862." Coach: "How many more churners does the care team reach?" (none: 98 vs 98) and "Would a different split agree?" The project's bootstrap answers it: the interval includes zero.

Checks for understanding

Ask these out loud during the demo and the lab:

  • Why can averaging logistic regression with boosting score below boosting alone? Answer: an equal vote gives a clearly weaker member the same say; the gain from its difference does not outweigh its weakness.
  • Where do a meta-model's training features come from? Answer: out-of-fold predictions on the training rows (cross_val_predict, or StackingClassifier's cv).
  • What do the meta-model's weights tell you about churn? Answer: nothing about customers; only how the members' opinions are combined.
  • Stack 98, booster 98 churners in the top 150. What would make you ship the stack? Answer: a gain that survives a bootstrap or cross-validation and changes a decision (more churners called), worth 19 fits and four pieces to run.

Workbook self-check, items Q1 to Q6.

Answers: Q1 B) About 0.86: members that make the same mistakes add almost nothing; Q2 [0.57 0.32] [2 0]; Q3 D) In-sample predictions are overconfident, so the meta-model over-trusts the most flexible member; Q4 A) Build the vote with voting="soft", from members that all have predict_proba; Q5 C) ValueError: every member needs a name, as a (string, estimator) pair; Q6 D) Keep the single booster: the gain is within the noise and changes no call, and the stack has 19 fits and four pieces to run. Model Selection and Learning Curves

65 min
Objectives (from the lesson page)
  • Compare several models fairly with cross_validate: the same preprocessing pipeline, the same folds and the same metric, reporting the spread and the fit time
  • Decide whether a gap between two models is bigger than the noise, using paired per-fold differences
  • Draw a validation curve with ValidationCurveDisplay and point to where a model moves from underfitting to overfitting
  • Draw a learning curve with LearningCurveDisplay and say whether more data or a more flexible model is the better next step
  • Make a final choice you can defend, then score it once on the untouched test set

Mental model to say

"Pinecrest's gear team is choosing a tent to stock. If they pitch tent A on a calm night and tent B in a storm, the result says more about the weather than the tents. A fair test pitches every tent at the same campsites on the same nights, many nights in a row, and compares them night by night." Then map it: campsites = folds, nights = repeats, leaks = scores.

Three questions, three tools: Which is best? Same pipeline, same folds, one metric chosen first, the spread next to every mean. Is the gap real? Paired per-fold differences. What would help? A validation curve (turn one knob) and a learning curve (add data). And one rule above all: the test set is opened once, after the choice.

  1. One recipe for every candidate (2m). "A fair contest starts with identical inputs: same split, same features, preprocessing written once as a function so each candidate gets a fresh copy." 
    import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.compose import ColumnTransformer
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline
from sklearn.impute import SimpleImputer
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler, OneHotEncoder

customers = pd.read_csv("data/customers_churn.csv")
numeric = ["tenure_months", "orders_12m", "spend_12m", "avg_order_value", "days_since_last_order",
           "returns_12m", "return_rate", "support_tickets_12m", "discount_share", "app_user",
           "email_opens_90d"]
categorical = ["home_store", "region", "age_group", "loyalty_tier", "preferred_category"]

X = customers[numeric + categorical]
y = customers["churned"]
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(X, y, test_size=0.25, stratify=y, random_state=42)

def make_preprocess():
    return ColumnTransformer([
        ("num", make_pipeline(SimpleImputer(strategy="median"), StandardScaler()), numeric),
        ("cat", make_pipeline(SimpleImputer(strategy="most_frequent"),
                              OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore")), categorical),
    ])

print(X_train.shape, X_test.shape)
print(f"churn rate in training data: {y_train.mean():.3f}")
    (4500, 16) (1500, 16)
churn rate in training data: 0.165
  2. Four candidates, one with no brain (1m). "The DummyClassifier is there on purpose: every comparison needs a floor." 
    from sklearn.dummy import DummyClassifier
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestClassifier, HistGradientBoostingClassifier

candidates = {
    "baseline": make_pipeline(make_preprocess(), DummyClassifier()),
    "logistic": make_pipeline(make_preprocess(), LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000)),
    "forest": make_pipeline(make_preprocess(), RandomForestClassifier(
        n_estimators=200, min_samples_leaf=5, n_jobs=-1, random_state=42)),
    "boosting": make_pipeline(make_preprocess(), HistGradientBoostingClassifier(random_state=42)),
}
print(list(candidates))
    ['baseline', 'logistic', 'forest', 'boosting']
  3. Ten scores each, on the same folds (4m). "cross_validate returns per-fold scores and fit times. One splitter object with a fixed random_state: the same campsites on the same nights for every tent." Start it, then talk while it runs (about half a minute). 
    from sklearn.model_selection import RepeatedStratifiedKFold, cross_validate

cv = RepeatedStratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, n_repeats=2, random_state=42)

fold_scores = {}
summary = []
for name, model in candidates.items():
    result = cross_validate(model, X_train, y_train, cv=cv, scoring="roc_auc")
    fold_scores[name] = result["test_score"]
    summary.append({"model": name,
                    "mean_auc": result["test_score"].mean(),
                    "std_auc": result["test_score"].std(),
                    "fit_seconds": result["fit_time"].mean()})

summary = pd.DataFrame(summary).set_index("model")
print(summary[["mean_auc", "std_auc"]].round(3))

  5. Paired differences (3m). "Subtract fold by fold: the shared ups and downs cancel." 
    def paired(a, b):
    diff = folds[a] - folds[b]
    print(f"{a} - {b}: mean {diff.mean():+.4f}, std {diff.std():.4f}, "
          f"{a} wins {(diff > 0).sum()} of {len(diff)} folds")

paired("boosting", "logistic")
paired("forest", "boosting")
    boosting - logistic: mean +0.0289, std 0.0113, boosting wins 10 of 10 folds
forest - boosting: mean +0.0070, std 0.0078, forest wins 9 of 10 folds
    Boosting beats logistic regression in 10 of 10 folds: real. The forest edges default boosting in 9 of 10 by less than 0.01: small but consistent. The std of the differences (0.008) is half the raw std: pairing removed noise.
  6. A validation curve: turn one knob (4m). "In a pipeline the knob is step name, two underscores, parameter." Show the chart; then read the table. 
    from sklearn.model_selection import StratifiedKFold, ValidationCurveDisplay
from sklearn.tree import DecisionTreeClassifier

tree_pipe = make_pipeline(make_preprocess(), DecisionTreeClassifier(random_state=42))
cv5 = StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=42)
depths = [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15]

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 4), layout="constrained")
vc = ValidationCurveDisplay.from_estimator(
    tree_pipe, X_train, y_train,
    param_name="decisiontreeclassifier__max_depth", param_range=depths,
    cv=cv5, scoring="roc_auc", score_name="ROC AUC", ax=ax,
)
ax.set_xlabel("max_depth")
ax.set_title("Deeper trees memorize the training data and stop generalizing")
plt.show()

curve = pd.DataFrame({"max_depth": depths,
                      "train_auc": vc.train_scores.mean(axis=1),
                      "validation_auc": vc.test_scores.mean(axis=1)})

  8. Make the call, then test once (2m). "Take the forest as the current best default; fit it on all training rows and open the test set, once." 
    from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score

final = candidates["forest"].fit(X_train, y_train)
test_auc = roc_auc_score(y_test, final.predict_proba(X_test)[:, 1])

print(f"cross-validated AUC: {summary.loc['forest', 'mean_auc']:.3f} +/- {summary.loc['forest', 'std_auc']:.3f}")
print(f"test AUC (one look): {test_auc:.3f}")

Three mistakes to expect, and how to coach each
  • Crowning a winner by the third decimal. "Forest 0.867, boosting 0.860: forest wins." Coach: "What is the spread? What do the paired differences say?" Teach the phrase "within noise" as a respectable conclusion.
  • A splitter per model, or the test set in the loop. Neither raises anything. Coach with the checklist on the board: one splitter object, created once; X_test appears in exactly one cell, at the end.
  • Reading the curves backwards. Learners say "logistic needs more data" because its score is lower. Coach: "Are the two curves still apart? Is validation still rising?" Flat and close = high bias = features or a more flexible model; a wide gap still closing = variance = regularize or add data.

Checks for understanding

Ask these out loud:

  • Why is the baseline's average precision 0.165? Answer: a random ordering finds churners at the base rate; AP of a constant score equals the positive share.
  • Mean gap 0.004, std of differences 0.012, A wins 6 of 10. Conclusion? Answer: within noise; choose on speed, simplicity or explainability.
  • What does "Test" mean in a ValidationCurveDisplay legend? Answer: the validation folds inside cross-validation, not X_test.
  • The test score lands 0.006 below the CV mean. What do you do? Answer: report it as is. It is within the CV range; changing the model now would need a new test set.

Closing

Exit ticket (3 questions, no notes, 4 minutes)
  1. Why must a stack's meta-model learn from out-of-fold predictions? Answer: in-sample predictions are overconfident (the booster scores 0.999 on its own training rows, 0.859 honestly), so the meta-model would over-trust the most flexible member and fail on new data.
  2. Two models: CV AUC 0.867 and 0.860; paired differences mean +0.007, std 0.008, the first wins 9 of 10 folds. Real? What else decides? Answer: small but consistent (wins nearly every fold; the mean is well above 0.008 / √10 = 0.0025). Then weigh cost, speed and explainability, and confirm once on the test set.
  3. Your final project: which problem, which deciding metric, and what does doing nothing score? Answer: for example "churn, average precision, 0.165 (contact everyone: -$7,620)"; "demand, MAE, per-product mean 13.42"; "fraud, average precision then cost, review nothing $24,000".
