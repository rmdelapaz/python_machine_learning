5.4

Voting and Stacking

By the end you can
  • Explain why a group of models beats its members only when their errors differ, and measure that diversity
  • Build hard and soft VotingClassifier ensembles from complete preprocessing pipelines
  • Build a stack by hand from out-of-fold predictions, then with StackingClassifier, and explain why in-sample predictions leak
  • Judge whether an ensemble's gain is real (bootstrap) and worth its cost in training time, latency and explainability
Key ideas
  • A crowd is wise only when its members disagree. Independent 70% voters reach 0.837 in fives; when most copy one opinion, adding voters barely helps.
  • Measure diversity on out-of-fold predictions (cross_val_predict), never on the test set. Forest and booster correlate 0.89 on churn: near-copies.
  • Soft voting averages probabilities and keeps predict_proba; hard voting counts labels and loses it. Choose weights out-of-fold.
  • Stacking fits a simple meta-model on out-of-fold member predictions; StackingClassifier(..., cv=cv) does it in one estimator. Its weights describe the combination, not the customers.
  • In-sample meta-features leak: the meta-model over-trusts the overconfident member and can score below its own best member, with no warning.
  • Blending uses one holdout instead of folds: simpler, noisier, natural for time-ordered data (blend on a later period).
  • Ship an ensemble only when the gain survives a bootstrap or CV and changes a decision, and is worth 19 fits and four pieces to run instead of one.
Worked example: a strong AND different member

Lesson 5.4's logistic regression was the weak, different member. What if it were strong AND different? Give it the shapes a straight line cannot draw (Lesson 2.3): a flag for customers quiet for more than 150 days, flags for very new and very old customers, and support tickets counted only for customers without the app.

import pandas as pd
from sklearn.compose import ColumnTransformer
from sklearn.ensemble import HistGradientBoostingClassifier
from sklearn.impute import SimpleImputer
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score
from sklearn.model_selection import StratifiedKFold, cross_val_predict, train_test_split
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import OneHotEncoder, StandardScaler

customers = pd.read_csv("data/customers_churn.csv")
numeric = ["tenure_months", "orders_12m", "spend_12m", "avg_order_value", "days_since_last_order",
           "returns_12m", "return_rate", "support_tickets_12m", "discount_share", "app_user", "email_opens_90d"]
categorical = ["home_store", "region", "age_group", "loyalty_tier", "preferred_category"]

def add_shapes(df):                       # the shapes a straight line cannot draw (Lesson 2.3)
    return df.assign(lapsed=(df["days_since_last_order"] > 150).astype(int),
                     new_cust=(df["tenure_months"] < 6).astype(int),
                     veteran=(df["tenure_months"] > 72).astype(int),
                     tickets_no_app=df["support_tickets_12m"] * (1 - df["app_user"]))

X = add_shapes(customers[numeric + categorical])
y = customers["churned"]
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(X, y, test_size=0.25, stratify=y, random_state=42)
shapes = ["lapsed", "new_cust", "veteran", "tickets_no_app"]
prep = ColumnTransformer([
    ("num", make_pipeline(SimpleImputer(strategy="median"), StandardScaler()), numeric + shapes),
    ("cat", make_pipeline(SimpleImputer(strategy="most_frequent"), OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore")), categorical)])
logreg_shapes = make_pipeline(prep, LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000))
boost = make_pipeline(ColumnTransformer([("cat", OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore"), categorical)],
                                        remainder="passthrough"), HistGradientBoostingClassifier(random_state=42))

cv = StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=42)
oof = pd.DataFrame({name: cross_val_predict(m, X_train, y_train, cv=cv, method="predict_proba")[:, 1]
                    for name, m in [("logreg_shapes", logreg_shapes), ("boost", boost)]})
print("out-of-fold ROC AUC:", {c: round(roc_auc_score(y_train, oof[c]), 3) for c in oof})
print("correlation:", round(oof.corr().iloc[0, 1], 3))
print("out-of-fold ROC AUC of the average:", round(roc_auc_score(y_train, oof.mean(axis=1)), 3))
out-of-fold ROC AUC: {'logreg_shapes': 0.877, 'boost': 0.862}
correlation: 0.878
out-of-fold ROC AUC of the average: 0.877

On its own the feature-built logistic regression (0.877 out-of-fold) beats the booster (0.862), and the two still disagree a fair amount (correlation 0.878). Yet their average adds nothing out-of-fold. Check it once on the test customers:

test = pd.DataFrame({name: m.fit(X_train, y_train).predict_proba(X_test)[:, 1]
                     for name, m in [("logreg_shapes", logreg_shapes), ("boost", boost)]})
for c in ["logreg_shapes", "boost"]:
    print(f"{c:14s} test ROC AUC {roc_auc_score(y_test, test[c]):.3f}")
print(f"{'soft vote':14s} test ROC AUC {roc_auc_score(y_test, test.mean(axis=1)):.3f}")
logreg_shapes  test ROC AUC 0.874
boost          test ROC AUC 0.866
soft vote      test ROC AUC 0.876

A gain of 0.002 over the best member. The big step came from the features, not the team: once one member knows the shapes, the other has little left to add. Ensembles polish; features transform.

6.1

Model Selection and Learning Curves

By the end you can
  • Compare several models fairly with cross_validate: the same preprocessing pipeline, the same folds and the same metric, reporting the spread and the fit time
  • Decide whether a gap between two models is bigger than the noise, using paired per-fold differences
  • Draw a validation curve with ValidationCurveDisplay and point to where a model moves from underfitting to overfitting
  • Draw a learning curve with LearningCurveDisplay and say whether more data or a more flexible model is the better next step
  • Make a final choice you can defend, then score it once on the untouched test set
Key ideas
  • Testing tents: same campsites (folds), same nights (repeats), compare night by night. One splitter object with a fixed random_state for every candidate.
  • cross_validate returns per-fold scores and fit times, for several metrics at once. Choose the deciding metric before looking.
  • Paired differences cancel shared fold difficulty. Worth acting on: the winner wins most folds and the mean difference is clearly larger than its std / √(number of folds). Otherwise: within noise.
  • Validation curve (one knob): both scores low = underfitting; training near perfect and validation falling = overfitting. Pipeline knobs are step__param.
  • Learning curve (more data): curves that meet and flatten = high bias (better features or a more flexible model); a wide gap still closing = variance (regularize or add data).
  • Within noise? Prefer the simpler model (the one-standard-error rule).
  • Open the test set once, after the choice, and report its score as is.
Worked example: features against a stronger model, fold by fold

Is the feature-built logistic regression really better than default boosting at the top of the list, or did it get one lucky split? Score both on the same 10 folds with average precision and compare fold by fold. (Run it after the 5.4 worked example, in the same notebook.)

from sklearn.model_selection import RepeatedStratifiedKFold, cross_val_score

rcv = RepeatedStratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, n_repeats=2, random_state=42)
scores = {name: cross_val_score(m, X_train, y_train, cv=rcv, scoring="average_precision")
          for name, m in [("logreg_shapes", logreg_shapes), ("boost", boost)]}
for name, s in scores.items():
    print(f"{name:14s} CV AP {s.mean():.3f} +/- {s.std():.3f}")
diff = scores["logreg_shapes"] - scores["boost"]
print(f"logreg_shapes - boost: mean {diff.mean():+.3f}, std {diff.std():.3f}, wins {(diff > 0).sum()} of 10")
logreg_shapes  CV AP 0.640 +/- 0.028
boost          CV AP 0.596 +/- 0.030
logreg_shapes - boost: mean +0.045, std 0.015, wins 10 of 10

A lead of 0.045 in every one of the 10 folds, with the differences varying by only 0.015: a real difference, not luck. A simple model with good features beat a flexible model without them, which is what a flat learning curve for logistic regression predicted.

Glossary

TermMeaning
ensembleA model made of several models whose predictions are combined
soft / hard votingAveraging probabilities / taking the majority of 0-1 labels
out-of-fold (OOF) predictionA prediction for a training row from a model fitted on the other folds
stackingA meta-model trained on the members' out-of-fold predictions
meta-model (final estimator)The second-level model in a stack; keep it simple
blendingStacking with a single holdout (for time data: a later period)
paired bootstrapResampling the same test rows for two models to see the spread of their difference
paired differenceScore of model A minus model B on the same fold
validation curveTraining and validation scores as one hyperparameter changes
learning curveTraining and validation scores as the amount of training data grows
high bias / high varianceUnderfitting (too simple) / overfitting (memorizing)
one-standard-error ruleChoose the simplest setting within one standard error of the best
