🧠 Advanced Machine Learning: Course Syllabus

An instructor-led course in 13 three-hour sessions. Go beyond supervised models on tables: explain what a model learned, find structure without labels, train neural networks with PyTorch, work with text and recommendations, ship a model behind an API, and finish with a capstone of your own.

📚 Course Description

This course picks up where Machine Learning Foundations ends. You already know how to frame a problem, split data honestly, build pipelines and tune trees and boosting. Here you learn to explain models and check them for fairness, segment customers and detect anomalies without labels, compress many features into a few, build and train neural networks on an ordinary laptop, classify text and recommend products, and make your work reproducible and deployable.

Every session uses the same company: Pinecrest Outfitters, a fictional outdoor-gear retailer, plus two public image datasets (Fashion-MNIST and EuroSAT) for the deep-learning sessions. The data hides real structure to discover: five customer segments, a fraud signal that only shows in combinations, a five-dimensional taste space behind purchases.

🎯 By the end you will be able to

  • Explain a model globally and locally with permutation importance, partial dependence, SHAP and LIME, and check it for unequal errors across groups
  • Segment customers with K-Means, hierarchical, density-based and mixture models, and choose the number of clusters with evidence
  • Detect anomalies and reduce dimensions with PCA, LDA, t-SNE and UMAP without over-reading the pictures
  • Build and train neural networks in PyTorch: dense networks, CNNs, LSTMs and transfer learning, on a CPU
  • Classify text with TF-IDF models and build recommenders evaluated with hit rate
  • Make experiments reproducible with MLflow, serve a model with FastAPI and watch for drift
  • Carry a capstone from question to a written model report

Prerequisite: Machine Learning Foundations (11 sessions) or equivalent.

✅ Prerequisites

  • Machine Learning Foundations or equivalent experience: scikit-learn pipelines, cross-validation, classification and regression metrics, and tree-based models including gradient boosting.
  • Python and pandas at the level of Python Data Science.
  • A Windows, macOS or Linux laptop with about 3 GB free (no GPU needed). The image datasets and pretrained weights (about 175 MB) download the first time they are used.

💡 Format

13 sessions × 3 hours = 39 class hours, two lessons per session (the studio sessions 12, 13 focus on one). Every lesson on the course site is written for a solo learner and takes about 90 to 120 minutes, so two lessons do not fit whole in one room session. The room protects what is hardest alone: the mental model, a live-coding demo you type along with, a guided lab with help on hand, and starting each lesson project. At home you finish the projects and do each lesson's exercises, quiz and journal entry on the free course site: about two to three hours between sessions.

Each session runs 0:00 to 3:00: a warm-up on last session, the first lesson, a 10-minute break, the second lesson, and a wrap-up with an exit ticket and the next bring list.

Twice weekly: about 7 weeks. Two homework sets a week, so plan on four to six hours outside class.
Weekly: 13 weeks, with a full week for each homework set.
Intensive days: two sessions a day; homework becomes evening practice and the capstone is finished afterward.

🏁 Checkpoint and Project

  • Self-checks and exit tickets in every session are practice, not grades: they tell you and your instructor what to revisit.
  • Checkpoint 1 (Session 6, 30 minutes): an individual, open-notebook task on explaining models, clustering and dimensionality reduction (Modules 7 to 9), scored with a short rubric.
  • Capstone. In Session 11 you choose one of five Pinecrest problems (demand forecasting, a fraud review queue, review triage, recommendations, or customer segments) and write a one-paragraph plan. Sessions 12 and 13 are studios with milestone check-ins, ending in a showcase. You hand in a notebook that reruns, a model card and a one-page recommendation. An honest "do not deploy" backed by evidence is a full answer.

🧰 Required Software and Data

  • Python 3.12 or newer: the same environment as Machine Learning Foundations
  • The course packages: python -m pip install -r requirements.txt (adds nothing new if you took Foundations): SHAP, LIME, UMAP, PyTorch and torchvision (CPU build), MLflow, FastAPI
  • JupyterLab or VS Code: as in Foundations
  • The course data: pinecrest-ml-data.zip, plus Fashion-MNIST, EuroSAT and two pretrained networks that PyTorch downloads on first use (about 175 MB)
  • No GPU, accounts or paid tools: every deep-learning session is sized to run on a laptop processor in minutes

Everything installs once, at home, before Session 1, following Lesson 0.1. Your instructor sends a setup guide and an environment check a week ahead.
